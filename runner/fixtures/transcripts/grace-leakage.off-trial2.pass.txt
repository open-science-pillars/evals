## Why this region breaks the product

**`knowledge/podaac/gotchas/grace-coastal-leakage.md`** (severity high, status stable, human-verified by PaulMRamirez 2026-07-04) names your case almost verbatim:

> Nearshore ocean mass series inherit land signal: apparent coastal sea level or ocean-mass trends **near Greenland**, Alaska, or major river basins can be dominated by leakage rather than ocean change. Nothing errors; the series just is not what its coordinates claim.

Its stated correct approach is to keep "a buffer from strong land sources or model them explicitly," and it closes: "Basin-scale open-ocean averages are robust; the trap is specifically the nearshore." Southeast Greenland shelf is the nearshore of the strongest land source in the Northern Hemisphere, with no buffer available.

Two independent things go wrong at once:

**1. The region is smaller than the measurement.** Per `knowledge/podaac/datasets/grace-fo-mascons.md`, the solution is 4,551 equal-area 3° spherical caps — about 112,000 km² each, roughly 335 km across — and the 0.5° grid only *represents* them: "the native information scale is the mascon, not the grid cell." The SE Greenland shelf is a strip on the order of 50–150 km wide. You'd be asking for a number at well under one-third of a resolution element. The dataset concept's Uncertainty section rules on this directly: a region "whose area approaches or falls below the mascon scale is resolution- and leakage-dominated."

**2. The contaminant is ~100× the signal.** Using the conversion in `knowledge/podaac/recipes/grace-mass-to-sea-level.md` (mass in Gt = anomaly in cm × area in km² × 1e-5), and taking the shelf as ~2×10⁵ km²: a genuine ocean-mass trend of 2 mm/yr water-equivalent is about **0.4 Gt/yr**. The adjacent SE Greenland ice sector is losing mass at order 10¹–10² Gt/yr. The CRI filter "reduces leakage substantially but does not eliminate it" — so even a residual of one percent of the land signal matches or exceeds the entire quantity you're trying to measure. That ratio is mine, on general method, not from a concept; but it's the reason no amount of careful masking rescues this.

A trend computed here would be numerically stable, plausible-looking, and would be measuring Helheim and Kangerdlugssuaq, not the ocean.

## Two things that are *not* problems here, for the record

- **The inter-mission gap doesn't bite.** The July 2017–May 2018 hole (plus August/September 2018) falls entirely before your window, so 2019–2024 needs no bridging — unusual good luck for a GRACE trend.
- **The GAD fix is avoidable, but only if you re-download.** The recipe's provenance notes a July 2025 correction to "the GAD mass added back to the **ocean part of land/ocean mascons**" — precisely your cells — carried only by files whose series extends past March 2025. The current file runs to 2026-07-16, so a fresh pull has it; a staged older download of a 2019–2024 window does not.

## What I'd compute instead

Which of these you want depends on the question under the question:

- **If you care about Greenland freshwater reaching the shelf** — this is what the product is *for*. The dataset concept records CMR listing it as "the recommended one for land-ice applications." Define the SE Greenland sector as whole **land** mascons, sum with true mascon areas, fit with annual and semi-annual terms per the recipe. That's a well-posed number with an honest uncertainty budget, and it's the physically meaningful driver of shelf freshening.
- **If you genuinely need ocean bottom pressure on the shelf** — GRACE cannot give it to you there at any processing level. That needs in-situ OBP recorders or a regional model; note ECCO v4r4 ends in 2017 and won't cover your window either.
- **If you want the damage quantified rather than argued** — the recipe prescribes differencing the CRI and unfiltered grids over the coastal mascons as the leakage bound. That converts "this is contaminated" into a number, and it's a legitimate deliverable. It needs Earthdata credentials and the MCP permission.
