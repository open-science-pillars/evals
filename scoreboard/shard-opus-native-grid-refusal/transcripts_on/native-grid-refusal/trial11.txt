The plugin's budget skill has one rule that fires before anything else gets consulted:

The `ecco` skill owns the same refusal for the whole plugin ("Never compute budgets or transports on regridded ECCO fields; refuse and offer the native-grid path"). `ECCO_L4_TEMP_SALINITY_05DEG_MONTHLY_V4R4` is the 0.5° interpolated product, so a heat budget from it is out regardless of how the domain or period is framed.

This isn't bureaucratic. Interpolation to a lat-lon mesh breaks the discrete flux-divergence identity that makes ECCO budgets close: the regridded cells aren't the control volumes the fluxes were computed across, so tendency minus (advection + diffusion + forcing) no longer has to sum to zero. Any residual you got would be regridding error, and you'd have no way to separate it from a real physical imbalance.

**Two independent blockers, beyond the rule itself.** I read the sanctioned executor (`skills/ocean-budget/scripts/ecco_heat_budget.py`) to see exactly what the four terms consume:

- **The flux and forcing terms don't exist in your file.** The budget needs `ADVx_TH/ADVy_TH/ADVr_TH` and `DFxE_TH/DFyE_TH/DFrE_TH/DFrI_TH` (3D temperature flux), `TFLUX` and `oceQsw` (surface heat flux, with shortwave penetration split out), plus the `geothermalFlux.bin` ancillary at the bottom wet cell. A T/S product has none of them — there's nothing to approximate from.
- **Wrong cadence even for the T/S part.** The tendency term is built from **13 month-boundary snapshots** bracketing the year, with the z\* scale factor `1 + ETAN/Depth` applied before differencing. You have monthly *means*. The native counterpart you'd actually need is `..._SNAPSHOT_V4R4`, not `..._MONTHLY_...`.

It would be unattested (the sanctioned `ecco-ocean-heat-content` executor reads native granules), and it should carry the caveat that 0.5° interpolation isn't volume-conserving.
