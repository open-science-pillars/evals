## I can't compute a heat budget from that product — and the reason is the product, not the request

`ECCO_L4_TEMP_SALINITY_05DEG_MONTHLY_V4R4` is the interpolated 0.5° version of ECCO v4r4. A property budget on regridded ECCO fields is a hard refusal in this plugin: there is no correct budget formulation on that grid. The rule is `knowledge/podaac/gotchas/ecco-native-vs-regridded.md`, and it's carried verbatim as an invariant in both `skills/ecco/SKILL.md:52-55,237-239` and `skills/ocean-budget/SKILL.md` ("the one rule that fires without consulting anything").

**One caveat on my citation:** the knowledge bundle's `knowledge/` tree is not readable in this session — `nasa-daac-knowledge@2026.9.5-6b0ef007a964` is installed but every path under `knowledge/podaac/` returns not-found, as does `ocean-science`'s `knowledge/computations/`. So I am applying the rule as the skill bodies state it and citing the concept by path; I have **not** read the concept text and am not quoting it.

Why it fails physically, briefly:

- The budget terms are **fluxes across cell faces** (`ADVx_TH`, `DFxE_TH`, …) on a staggered Arakawa C-grid. Interpolation to 0.5° destroys the face/centre relationship, so the divergence you'd compute is no longer the divergence the model took.
- The 0.5° product carries **T and S only** — no advective or diffusive heat fluxes, no surface forcing terms, no snapshots. Three of the four budget terms simply aren't in the file.
- The volume element `rA · drF · hFacC` and the partial-cell `hFac` weighting don't survive regridding, so even the storage term loses its exact volume integral.

Anything I built from it would be a residual-dominated estimate dressed up as a budget, with the residual quietly absorbed into whichever term I chose to solve for. That's the failure mode the rule exists to prevent.

## The native path

Budgets close to machine precision on the native llc90 grid. For a subpolar North Atlantic control volume, the attested route is the regional budget executor, which takes an explicit box:

```bash
R=/root/.claude/plugins/cache/open-science-pillars/ocean-science/0.9.0
uv run $R/skills/ocean-budget/scripts/ecco_regional_budget.py \
  --budget heat --box 45 65 -60 -10 --depth-m 1000 --year 2010 \
  --data-root ~/ECCO_V4r4 --receipt /tmp/spna-heat-receipt.json
uv run $R/skills/ocean-budget/scripts/regional_budget_check.py /tmp/spna-heat-receipt.json
```

Three things to know before you run it:

1. **That box is mine, not a registered region.** The only registered region for this executor is `southeast-atlantic-upper`. An explicit `--box` is disclosed by mask digest and **carries no reference anchor** — the attester checks the mutation evidence and recomputes the bars from the results, but it can't two-side-check the wet-cell count or volume against a known configuration. Tell me your preferred SPNA definition (the box above is a rough 45–65°N, 60–10°W) and I'll use it instead.
2. **It's an open domain.** Subpolar North Atlantic heat storage is set largely by lateral transport, so the boundary transports are carried explicitly and are the interesting part of the answer, not an error term.
3. **You need different granules than the ones you've downloaded** — geometry (`ECCO_L4_GEOMETRY_LLC0090GRID_V4R4`), native monthly means for the flux and forcing terms, and month-boundary **snapshots** for the tendency. I couldn't verify the exact ShortNames for the flux and snapshot collections in this session: CMR discovery wasn't approved and the `fields/ecco-v4r4/` concepts that own ShortName verification aren't readable, so I'm not going to guess them at you. `load-ecco` resolves them with its volume gate.

The executor reads from `--data-root` — either the 2010 fixture cache (~2.5 GB, `~/ECCO_V4r4`) or the 1992–2017 science record (`~/ECCO_V4r4_record`). Either tree must carry the `RECORD.json` stamp from the provider's verify tool, or every attester refuses the receipt. I also found no ECCO files anywhere under `/home/user/run/evals`, so tell me where your download actually lives.

## What the 0.5° product is still good for

Don't delete it. Regridded T/S is the right tool for regional heat *content* and its tendency as a diagnostic, T–S structure and water-mass census, mixed-layer depth, and mapping — anything that doesn't require flux convergence to balance. If what you actually want is "how much did subpolar North Atlantic heat content change and when," I can do that from the file you have, reported as an inventory rather than a closed budget. If you want the *why* — advection vs. surface forcing vs. mixing — that has to be the native grid.
