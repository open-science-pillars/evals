## The refusal

`ECCO_L4_TEMP_SALINITY_05DEG_MONTHLY_V4R4` is the regridded (interpolated) ECCO product. A heat budget on regridded fields is a hard refusal in the `ocean-science:ocean-budget` workflow — it fires before any computation, under any framing of the request. The cited concept is gotcha **`ecco-native-vs-regridded`**, owned by the `ocean-science:ecco` skill.

The reason it's a refusal and not a warning: the budget terms are flux divergences defined on the Arakawa C-grid staggering. Advective and diffusive fluxes live on cell faces, tracers on cell centers, and the closure is an exact telescoping sum over the native LLC90 control volumes (`rA * drF * hFacC`). Interpolating T and S to a 0.5° lat-lon mesh destroys that discrete identity — the fluxes that would close the budget were never regridded with it, and no correct budget formulation exists on the target grid. What you'd get is a residual dominated by interpolation error, with no way to distinguish that from missing physics. There's no correction factor that recovers it.

Also worth noting independently: the 0.5° product carries T and S only. Even setting the grid problem aside, it contains none of the flux terms a budget needs — no `ADVx_TH`/`ADVy_TH`/`ADVr_TH`, no `DFxE_TH`/`DFrI_TH`, no `TFLUX`, and no snapshots for the tendency bookends. Three of the four terms simply aren't in the file.

## The native path

The attested route for a regional heat budget is the sanctioned executor, which takes an explicit box:

```bash
R=/root/.claude/plugins/cache/open-science-pillars/ocean-science/0.9.0
uv run $R/skills/ocean-budget/scripts/ecco_regional_budget.py \
  --budget heat --box 50 65 -60 -10 --depth-m <depth> --year <YYYY> \
  --data-root ~/ECCO_V4r4 --receipt /tmp/spna-heat-receipt.json
uv run $R/skills/ocean-budget/scripts/regional_budget_check.py /tmp/spna-heat-receipt.json
```

Two things to flag on that. Only `southeast-atlantic-upper` is a registered region, so a subpolar box is disclosed by mask digest and carries no reference anchor — the attester checks the mutation oracles and recomputes the bars, but not the two-sided reference checks. And the subpolar gyre is an **open domain**: the domain-integrated closure holds only with the lateral boundary transports carried explicitly as a term, not folded into the residual.

The native collections this needs (standard ECCO v4r4 budget set — the authoritative list is in `knowledge/computations/ecco-regional-heat-budget.md`, which I could not read; the sandbox blocked the knowledge tree, so confirm against it before fetching):

- `ECCO_L4_GEOMETRY_LLC0090GRID_V4R4` — `rA`, `drF`, `hFacC`
- `ECCO_L4_TEMP_SALINITY_LLC0090GRID_SNAPSHOT_V4R4` — `THETA` tendency bookends
- `ECCO_L4_SEA_SURFACE_HEIGHT_LLC0090GRID_SNAPSHOT_V4R4` — `ETAN`, for the s\* free-surface scaling
- `ECCO_L4_OCEAN_3D_TEMPERATURE_FLUX_LLC0090GRID_MONTHLY_V4R4` — advective and diffusive terms
- `ECCO_L4_HEAT_FLUX_LLC0090GRID_MONTHLY_V4R4` — `TFLUX`, `oceQsw`
- `ECCO_L4_FRESH_WATER_FLUX_LLC0090GRID_MONTHLY_V4R4` — `oceFWflx`

## What the 0.5° product *is* good for

Don't throw it away — it's the right product for a subpolar **ocean heat content** diagnostic: volume-integrated OHC over 50–65°N, 60°W–10°W, its seasonal cycle and its trend. That's an integral of a state variable, which regridding degrades mildly and predictably, rather than a closure identity, which regridding breaks. It gives you the tendency — the left-hand side alone — with no attribution to advection, diffusion, or surface forcing.
