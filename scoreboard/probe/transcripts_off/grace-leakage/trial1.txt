## The problem is resolution, not method

`knowledge/podaac/datasets/grace-fo-mascons.md` (stable, human-verified 2026-09-13): the JPL solution estimates mass on **4,551 equal-area 3° spherical caps**. The 0.5° grid is a *representation* of those caps — "the native information scale is the mascon, not the grid cell."

Working that out: 4πR²/4551 ≈ **112,000 km² per mascon**, about 335 km on a side.

The SE Greenland shelf, Denmark Strait to Cape Farewell, is roughly 950 km of margin by 100–150 km of shelf width — call it **120,000 km²**. So the entire feature you asked about is *about one mascon in area*, shaped as a strip roughly 0.4 mascons wide across-margin. There is no ocean-only mascon anywhere on that shelf. Every mascon covering it straddles the coast, and what sits on the land side is the Helheim–Kangerlussuaq sector, the fastest-thinning part of the ice sheet.

The dataset concept names this case directly: "a basin whose area approaches or falls below the mascon scale is resolution- and leakage-dominated."
