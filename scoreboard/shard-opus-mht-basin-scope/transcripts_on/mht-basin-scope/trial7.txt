**ECCO v4r4, Atlantic at 26.5°N, 2010: 0.666 PW.** That is the RAPID-comparable number.

## Why the distinction is the whole answer

The attested computation here (`ecco-section-transport`, status `stable`, human-verified three times, `knowledge/computations/ecco-section-transport.md`) registers the section `global-26.5n` as **the closed global latitude circle**, 360 faces. Its recorded 2010 reference run is a heat transport mean of **+1.0963 PW**, against an independent `ecco_v4_py` implementation's 1.098 PW — a 0.002 PW cross-implementation agreement.

That is the Atlantic *plus* the Pacific plus the Indian contributions at that latitude. RAPID-MOCHA measures the Atlantic alone, Florida to Africa. The draft MHT concept (`knowledge/computations/ecco-mht-26n.md`, `status: draft`, no sanctioned executor yet) states the scope rule explicitly: with `basin` unset the computation means the full latitude circle, **"never a RAPID-comparable number"** — and it records the 2010 anchors as Atlantic `atlExt` **0.666 PW**, global circle **1.098 PW**, equal to the basin sum.

The trap is that the wrong number looks *better*. RAPID's published Atlantic MHT at 26.5°N is order 1.2–1.3 PW (my own background, **not** from a concept I read here — I'm not citing it and not differencing against it). The global-circle 1.098 PW would sit far closer to that than ECCO's actual Atlantic 0.666 PW does, so a scope error would read as good agreement while hiding a large low bias.

Caveats that ride with the number:
- **0.666 PW is draft-tier**, from a concept with no executor yet and no verified event — voiced as unreviewed. The stable, attested value in this package is the global-circle one.
- **Single year vs. multi-year.** 2010 alone sits in a wider envelope than RAPID's multi-year mean; 2010 is inside RAPID's coverage, so the periods do overlap.
- **Mass balance.** The global circle carries a net volume transport of −0.43 Sv in the same reference run, so strictly it is a temperature transport relative to a reference, not a heat transport. The Atlantic section is not mass-balanced either; any quoted difference needs the reference temperature stated.
- ECCO v4r4 ships **no formal error fields**, so there is no model-side uncertainty to combine.
