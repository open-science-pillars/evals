## The scope trap

There are two different 2010 numbers at 26.5°N, and only one is RAPID-comparable:

| Scope | 2010 mean | RAPID-comparable? |
|---|---|---|
| Full latitude circle (no basin mask) | **1.098 PW** | **No** |
| Atlantic (`atlExt`) | **0.666 PW** | Yes |

`knowledge/computations/ecco-mht-26n.md` (ocean-science, status **draft**) states the discipline explicitly: `basin` unset means the full latitude circle, "never a RAPID-comparable number," and records both anchors with global = the basin sum. The independent implementation behind the stable section-transport concept (`verification/transport_analysis.py`) carries the same two constants and labels `atlExt` "the RAPID-comparable scope."

The 1.098 PW figure is well-attested — `knowledge/computations/ecco-section-transport.md` (status **stable**, human-verified three times) records a 2010 reference run of the closed 26.5°N circle at **+1.0963 PW** against the golden's 1.098. It's just a global number. RAPID measures the Atlantic only; 1.098 PW includes the Pacific and Indian sectors, so quoting it against RAPID would be a basin-scope error, not a model bias.

## Provenance caveats on that headline

Three things weaken it, and you should know all three:

1. **Nothing was computed this session.** `~/ECCO_V4r4`, `~/ECCO_V4r4_record` and `~/RAPID_26N` are all absent. The 0.666 PW is a *recorded anchor*, not a fresh run.

A one-year mean gets a wider envelope than the band implies, so "ECCO runs low at 26.5°N" is the right reading, but 2010 alone doesn't quantify by how much.
