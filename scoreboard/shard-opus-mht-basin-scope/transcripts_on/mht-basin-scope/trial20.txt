## ECCO MHT at 26.5°N, 2010

**Headline: ECCO is about 0.7 PW low against RAPID — 0.67 PW vs 1.33 PW.**

The number that matters is the **Atlantic-masked** one:

| scope | 2010 mean |
|---|---|
| Atlantic (`basin_name="atlExt"`) — RAPID-comparable | **0.666 PW** |
| full latitude circle (no basin mask) | 1.098 PW |

The full-circle 1.098 PW is *not* the RAPID comparison. `calc_meridional_heat_trsp(ds, lat_vals=26.5)` with no `basin_name` integrates Atlantic + Pacific + Indian; RAPID/MOCHA observes the Atlantic section only. Quoting 1.098 PW against RAPID's ~1.3 PW would fake near-agreement and hide the real offset — that's a high-severity, human-verified trap in the bundle (`knowledge/podaac/gotchas/ecco-mht-basin-scope.md`, stable). The circle decomposes exactly as atlExt 0.666 + pacExt 0.430 + indExt 0.002 = 1.098, which is what the full-circle number is good for: a method check.

**Against RAPID.** RAPID/MOCHA observes ~1.3 PW mean with ~0.4 PW temporal standard deviation (Johns et al. 2011, 2004–2007: 1.33 ± 0.40 PW; published measurement uncertainty ~±0.2 PW on 10-day values). ECCO 2010 Atlantic sits ~0.66 PW below that — bigger than both the observed temporal spread and the array's measurement error, so it's a genuine offset, but not an anomalous one: the recipe states plainly that ECCO v4 Atlantic means sit low of the observed mean.

Two caveats that the single number hides, both required by the comparison discipline:
- **Periods don't match.** A single ECCO year against a 2004–2007 observed mean. 2010 is also the documented AMOC-minimum year, so part of that 0.66 PW is real ocean, not model bias. ECCO's 2010 Atlantic value falls below the recipe's 0.8–1.4 PW *multi-year* Atlantic band for exactly that reason — single years carry a wider envelope.
- ECCO v4r4 ships no formal error fields, so there's no model-side uncertainty to put on the 0.67.
