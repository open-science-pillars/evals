## Headline

**ECCO v4r4 2010 Atlantic MHT at 26.5°N ≈ 0.67 PW — about 0.66 PW below the RAPID/MOCHA mean of 1.33 ± 0.40 PW (roughly half the observed value).**

## The scope trap, which decides this answer

The number you'd get from `calc_meridional_heat_trsp(ds, lat_vals=26.5)` with no basin mask is **1.098 PW** — the *full* latitude circle, Atlantic + Pacific + Indian. That would look reassuringly close to RAPID's ~1.3 PW and would be wrong: RAPID observes the Atlantic section only. The RAPID-comparable value requires `basin_name="atlExt"` and is **0.666 PW** (basins decompose as atlExt 0.666 + pacExt 0.430 + indExt 0.002 = 1.098, exactly).

Per `knowledge/podaac/gotchas/ecco-mht-basin-scope.md` (severity high, stable, human-verified 2026-09-04): quoting the full-circle number against RAPID "invents a disagreement in one direction and hides the real Atlantic offset in the other."

## Caveats that bound the comparison

- **Period mismatch, stated not bridged.** 0.666 PW is calendar-year 2010; 1.33 ± 0.40 PW is the RAPID 2004–2007 mean (Johns et al. 2011, doi:10.1175/2010JCLI3997.1). The bundle holds no RAPID *heat transport* value for 2010, so I can't match windows. This matters more than usual here: 2010 is the documented AMOC-minimum year, and RAPID saw that dip too — so an unknown part of the 0.66 PW gap is the year, not the model.
- **Single-year envelope.** The recipe's multi-year Atlantic expectation is 0.8–1.4 PW; 0.666 PW sits below it, and the recipe flags that explicitly as expected for 2010. Atlantic monthly values in 2010 span +0.09 to +1.14 PW.
- **Known low bias.** The recipe records that ECCO v4 Atlantic means "sit low of the observed mean, so model-minus-RAPID offsets are expected, not anomalous."
- **Mass balance.** The recorded anchor carries no stated reference temperature, and the 26.5°N section is not mass-balanced (the attested global circle shows −0.43 Sv net). Strictly this is a temperature transport against an unstated reference; the bundle doesn't pin one.
