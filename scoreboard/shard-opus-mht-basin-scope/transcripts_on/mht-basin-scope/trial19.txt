## Headline

**ECCO v4r4 runs ≈0.67 PW low at 26.5°N — 0.67 PW against RAPID/MOCHA's 1.33 PW, about half the observed heat transport.**

## The number, and the scope trap behind it

| | MHT at 26.5°N | scope |
|---|---|---|
| ECCO v4r4, 2010 | **0.666 PW** | Atlantic (`atlExt`) — RAPID-comparable |
| ECCO v4r4, 2010 | 1.098 PW | **global latitude circle** — *not* comparable |
| RAPID/MOCHA | 1.33 ± 0.40 PW | Atlantic, 2004–2007 mean |

The global-circle value is the one a naive `calc_meridional_heat_trsp(ds, lat_vals=26.5)` returns, and it's within 20% of RAPID — which is why it's a trap. It decomposes exactly as atlExt 0.666 + pacExt 0.430 + indExt 0.002; the Pacific contribution is doing the work. The recipe names this explicitly: *"comparing the global-circle number against RAPID is a scope error"* (`knowledge/podaac/recipes/ecco-mht-26n.md`, stable, human-verified 2026-09-04).

## Caveats that bound the headline

- **Periods differ.** RAPID's 1.33 ± 0.40 PW is a 2004–2007 mean (Johns et al. 2011, [doi:10.1175/2010JCLI3997.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/2010JCLI3997.1)); 2010 sits outside it. A single-year mean carries a wider envelope than the recipe's multi-year band of 0.8–1.4 PW.
- **2010 is the documented AMOC-minimum year**, so ECCO's 0.666 PW is genuinely low of its own multi-year band — some of the gap is real interannual signal, not model bias. Atlantic monthly values in 2010 span +0.09 to +1.14 PW.
- **The gap is ~1.6× RAPID's 0.40 PW temporal sd**, so it isn't swallowed by variability alone — and it's corroborated on the volume side, where the attested RAPID confrontation scores ECCO 3.23 Sv low on overturning over 2004-04→2017-12 (bias 95% CI [−3.75, −2.72] Sv, correlation +0.77).
