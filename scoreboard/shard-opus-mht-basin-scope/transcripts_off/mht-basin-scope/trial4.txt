## The part that survives, and it's the real answer to your question

Even with data and a shell, **the attested route would not have produced a RAPID-comparable number**, and this is the thing worth knowing:

- `ecco-mht-26n` — meridional heat transport at 26.5°N — is a **draft contract with no sanctioned executor**. It doesn't run.
- The attested substitute is the heat transport across the registered section `global-26.5n`, which is the **closed global latitude circle**. That includes the Pacific and Indian Ocean contributions.
- RAPID-MOCHA measures the **Atlantic only**, Florida to Africa. The package knows that scope — it's a registered parameter (`--scope atlantic`) — but only on `ecco-amoc-26n`, which returns overturning in Sv, not heat transport in PW.

So a global 26.5°N MHT against RAPID's Atlantic MHT is a basin-scope mismatch, not a model-skill comparison. That's a difference of order the signal itself, not a caveat you can footnote.

Two further constraints that would bind the real run: the global circle is closed and near mass-balanced, which is what makes its heat transport well defined; an Atlantic-only section carries net throughflow, so an Atlantic MHT is a *temperature* transport and needs a stated reference temperature. And 2010 is a single year inside RAPID's record (which starts 2004), so it gets the wider single-year envelope, not the multi-year one.
