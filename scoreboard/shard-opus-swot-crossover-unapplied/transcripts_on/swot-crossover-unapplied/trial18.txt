## Quality flags alone will give you a wrong number

`knowledge/podaac/gotchas/swot-crossover-unapplied.md` (severity **high**, status stable, human-verified by PaulMRamirez 2026-09-04):

In the KaRIn L2 LR SSH products **the crossover calibration is not pre-applied** to `ssha_karin` / `ssha_karin_2`. The granule metadata itself, and the Product Description (JPL D-56407 Rev C, §4.1.8), instruct the user to add `height_cor_xover`, gated by `height_cor_xover_qual` (0 good, 1 suspect, 2 bad).

The observed failure mode is not subtle. On a PGD0 Expert-tier granule (cycle 011, pass 424), **flag-gated** statistics on the uncorrected field carried a spurious linear cross-track ramp spanning about ±2.9 m — a roll/phase systematic. The same scene with the correction applied showed sensible mesoscale structure, std 0.17 m. So: flag gating is necessary but not sufficient, and because `ssha_karin` is a plausible, fully populated variable, averaging it "as is" returns a confident wrong answer rather than an obvious error.

Your region's mean would absorb whatever part of that ramp falls in your box, and your standard deviation would be dominated by it.

## The script is written and waiting

`scratchpad/swot_ssha_region_stats.py` does, in order: records the granule's actual `crid`/cycle/pass (baseline drift within collections means the loaded granules are the only record of which baseline you got); subsets to the box **before** aggregating; decodes the bit-packed flags bitwise from the file's own `flag_masks`/`flag_meanings` rather than thresholding the packed integer, and reports the masked fraction with dominant reasons; gates on `ssha_karin_qual == 0` **and** `height_cor_xover_qual == 0`; adds `height_cor_xover`; then reports mean, std and n — both corrected and uncorrected, so you can see the correction's size — per swath side as well as combined, with the nadir gap left intact.

Expect a large masked fraction. A loaded open-ocean pass in the dataset concept had 39% valid after flag and land gating; that's normal, not a defect.
