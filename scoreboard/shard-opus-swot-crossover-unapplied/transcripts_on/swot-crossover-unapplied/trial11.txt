## The main thing: quality flags alone will not make this right

The correction you'd be missing is bigger than the signal you're measuring.

**`knowledge/podaac/gotchas/swot-crossover-unapplied.md`** (stable, severity high, human-verified 2026-09-04): in the KaRIn L2 LR SSH products the crossover calibration is **not pre-applied** to `ssha_karin` / `ssh_karin`. The granule metadata itself instructs adding `height_cor_xover`, and the Product Description (JPL D-56407 Rev C, §4.1.8) says the correction "is not applied in forming `ssh_karin`, `ssh_karin_2`, `ssha_karin`, or `ssha_karin_2`" and "should be added by the user."

What that does to exactly the statistic you asked for, observed on a PGD0 Expert-tier granule (cycle 011, pass 424): the flag-gated but uncorrected field carries a spurious linear cross-track ramp spanning about **±2.9 m**, a roll/phase systematic. The corrected field on the same scene gave **std 0.17 m** of sensible mesoscale structure. So a mean and std from `ssha_karin` masked on `ssha_karin_qual` alone is a confident, plausible-looking wrong answer — an order of magnitude too large in the std, and a mean that depends on where your box sits across the swath.

The correct field is `ssha_karin + height_cor_xover`, with `height_cor_xover_qual == 0` in the gate alongside `ssha_karin_qual` (0 good, 1 suspect, 2 bad).

## Ready to run

`swot_ssha_region_stats.py` in the scratchpad does it properly:

- reads `flag_masks`/`flag_meanings` **off the granule** rather than from a remembered bit layout, and gates on `== 0` (never thresholds a packed QA integer — core `quality-control`, check 5)
- subsets to the box **before** aggregating, reports what each mask dropped and the retained fraction (~39% valid on an open-ocean pass is normal, not a defect)
- reports corrected vs uncorrected side by side so you can see the ramp's size on *your* scene
- splits left/right swath — independent error profiles — and never touches the nadir gap
- records the `crid` global attribute, which is the only record of which processing baseline your granule came from
- prints the uncertainty caveat: `ssh_karin_uncert` is per-sample random noise only and excludes the correlated swath-scale systematics that dominate a regional average, so it is not the error bar on your mean. Neither is std/√n — SSHA is mesoscale-autocorrelated, so the effective sample size is far below n.
