## Quality flags alone will give you a wrong mean

You asked to use the quality flags to keep only good data. That's necessary and not sufficient. On SWOT KaRIn **L2_LR_SSH, `ssha_karin` arrives with the crossover calibration unapplied.** The correction ships as a separate variable, `height_cor_xover`, which you add to the anomaly yourself, gated by its own `height_cor_xover_qual`.

This matters specifically for *your* statistic. The uncorrected error is the KaRIn roll/phase error — large-scale, correlated across and along track. It is not noise that averages away over a region; it's close to a per-pass offset and tilt, so it lands more or less intact in the mean. Every pixel can pass `ssha_karin_qual == 0` and the regional mean still be wrong. The flags gate pixel-level defects; they say nothing about whether a calibration has been applied to the pixels that survive.

The standard deviation is the less-damaged of your two numbers, but it still inflates, since the uncorrected cross-track tilt shows up as spread.

So the gating I've implemented is:

- `ssha_karin_qual == 0` — bit-packed, strict good
- `ancillary_surface_classification_flag == 0` — open ocean only
- `height_cor_xover_qual == 0` — **because you can't apply a correction without gating it**
- then `ssha_karin + height_cor_xover`

Three smaller things the script also handles: the region mask is built on the 2-D `latitude`/`longitude` arrays, not `.sel()` (swath geometry isn't a regular grid); SWOT longitudes are 0–360 and get normalised before comparison; and left/right swaths are reported separately with the nadir gap left as the real void it is.

Two caveats it prints with the result. The std is spatial spread, not a standard error — KaRIn errors are correlated, so `std/sqrt(n)` badly understates uncertainty on the mean. And pixel posting is nominally uniform on the ground, so the unweighted pixel mean is right; `cos(lat)` weighting is a regular-grid habit and would be wrong here.
