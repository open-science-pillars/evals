## The thing that matters more than the flags

Your request was "use the quality flags to keep only good data." Flag-gating is necessary but **not sufficient** for this product, and the gap bites hardest on exactly the two numbers you asked for.

SWOT KaRIn L2 LR SSH ships the **crossover calibration correction unapplied**. `ssha_karin` as written in the file still carries the cross-track/long-wavelength systematic error that the correction removes; the fix travels in the granule as **`height_cor_xover`**, added to the height, and it has **its own quality flag** (`height_cor_xover_qual`) that gates it independently of `ssha_karin_qual`. A pixel can pass the SSHA flag and still have no usable crossover correction.

So `ssha_karin[qual == 0].mean()` and `.std()` is the wrong answer: the mean picks up a residual pass-dependent bias, and the **standard deviation is inflated**, because cross-swath systematic error is precisely the structure the correction takes out. My script computes both and prints them side by side so you can see the size of the effect on your box.

Two framing notes it prints with the result: KaRIn pixels are near-uniformly posted, so these are unweighted pixel statistics (no `cos(lat)` weighting); and the std is **spatial variability across one pass**, not an error bar — neighbouring pixels are correlated, so `std/√n` is not the standard error of the mean.
