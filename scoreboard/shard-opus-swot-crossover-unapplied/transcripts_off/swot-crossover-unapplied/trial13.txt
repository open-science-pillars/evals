## The part that matters: quality flags alone will give you a wrong answer

Your framing — "use the quality flags to keep only good data" — is the standard approach and it is **necessary but not sufficient** for L2 LR SSH. `ssha_karin` ships with the **crossover calibration not applied**. The correction rides along in a separate variable, `height_cor_xover`, and you have to subtract it yourself:

```python
ssha_calibrated = ssha_karin - height_cor_xover
```

This is not a rounding detail. `height_cor_xover` is a systematic, spatially-correlated term of order centimeters that varies along-track and across the swath. If you gate `ssha_karin_qual == 0` and take statistics on the raw field, you get a **biased mean** and an **inflated standard deviation** — and it looks perfectly clean, because every pixel passed its flag.

It also has its **own** quality flag, `height_cor_xover_qual`, which `ssha_karin_qual` does not gate. "Good data" therefore means both flags clear, not one. The script drops these two categories separately so you can see how many pixels each one costs.

Other things the script handles that commonly bite here: SWOT stores longitude in 0–360 (your box is probably −180–180); `ancillary_surface_classification_flag == 0` for open ocean; the two swaths reported separately since their error profiles are independent; and no `cos(lat)` weighting, because KaRIn posts on a near-equal-area ~2 km grid — that weighting is a lon-lat-grid habit that's wrong to import here. It reads the flag bit meanings out of the file's own attributes rather than hardcoding them, and prints the `crid` so you know which processing baseline you actually got.
