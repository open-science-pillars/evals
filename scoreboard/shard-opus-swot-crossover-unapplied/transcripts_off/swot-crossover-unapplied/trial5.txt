Run `--inspect` first — it prints the granule's own `flag_meanings`/`flag_masks` for every `*_qual` variable, which is what tells us whether `qual == 0` is the right gate for your tier and baseline:

```
./swot_ssha_region_stats.py YOUR_GRANULE.nc --inspect
./swot_ssha_region_stats.py YOUR_GRANULE.nc --bbox -75 33 -60 42
```

What it does, and deliberately doesn't:

- Decodes the packed flag bitwise from the file's CF attributes; never thresholds the packed integer numerically.
- Trims to the region *before* aggregating (a granule is a whole pass).
- Reports masked fraction with the dominant flag reasons, and keeps the two swaths separate — independent error profiles, and the nadir gap is never interpolated across.
- Applies `qual == 0` as an explicitly labelled **assumption**, not a cited fact, and prints the flag table beside it so you can check.
- Prints whether `height_cor_xover` is present but **does not apply it**, because I can't cite its quality gate.

Two things to flag on the statistic itself. The std it returns is spatial variability of SSHA across your box, not measurement uncertainty — the granule's own uncertainty variables are a separate quantity and the script lists them. And an unweighted mean is only defensible because KaRIn pixels are near-uniform in area on the swath grid; if your box clips one swath much more than the other, the two per-swath means are the more honest read.
