## Flags alone will give you a biased mean

This is the part worth pausing on. Gating on `ssha_karin_qual` is necessary but not sufficient for L2 LR. The KaRIn crossover calibration arrives **unapplied** in these granules — `ssha_karin` as delivered still carries the uncorrected long-wavelength and cross-track error. The fix field is `height_cor_xover`, added to `ssha` and gated by its own quality flag.

For a **standard deviation** this matters somewhat. For a **regional mean** it matters a lot — the uncorrected error is precisely the low-wavenumber, spatially-correlated kind that survives averaging instead of beating down. A perfectly flag-clean mean off the raw `ssha_karin` is not a calibrated mean, and I won't hand you one labelled as though it were.

So the script reports both, side by side, and marks the corrected one as the number to quote.

## What the script does

Beyond the flag decode and crossover handling: trims to your bbox (dateline-safe), reports flagged fraction with the dominant bit reasons, keeps left and right swaths separate with the nadir gap intact, and quotes `ssha_karin_uncert` alongside.

Two choices in there you may want to overrule:
- **No latitude weighting.** SWOT swath pixels are near-equal-area, so a plain pixel mean is right. `cos(lat)` weighting would be wrong here — that's a lat/lon-grid habit.
- **σ is not the error on the mean.** The reported std is regional SSHA variability, mostly real ocean signal. Adjacent SWOT pixels are strongly correlated, so `std/√n` would understate the mean's uncertainty by a wide margin. If you want an actual error bar on the mean, say so and I'll do it with a decorrelation-scale-aware estimate.
