## What the trend step would be

Whatever series you end up with, the trend goes through `ecco_trend_ci.py`, never a bare `polyfit`. Reading its header: it deseasonalizes by monthly climatology *jointly* with the trend (Frisch-Waugh-Lovell), because subtracting a climatology first hands `143/(144Y² - 1)` of the trend to the climatology; then OLS, lag-1 residual autocorrelation, `n_eff = n(1-r₁)/(1+r₁)` capped at `n`, and a 95% interval from Student's t on `n_eff - 2`.

Your period is favorable for this: 1992-01 to 2017-12 is 312 months, a multiple of 12, so `--deseasonalize climatology` is valid (it requires complete years). And `sea-level-analysis` makes it a hard refusal — "Never quote a rate without a CI and its period" — so the answer to "is there a trend" is a rate with an interval, not a yes/no.

Two framings that will apply to the result: ECCO is a dynamically consistent state estimate with **no formal error fields**, not an observational product; and a regional SSH trend over 26 years needs the internal-variability caveat, since dynamic decadal variability is typically the dominant regional signal.
