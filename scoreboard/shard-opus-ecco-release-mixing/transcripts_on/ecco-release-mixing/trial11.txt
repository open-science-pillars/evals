All four cover the full period, so "use whatever collections cover it" would sweep in both releases. `knowledge/podaac/gotchas/ecco-release-mixing.md` (severity **high**, stable, human-verified) says a series assembled from a mix of V4R4 and V4R4B folds the baseline correction into the signal — an apparent step or drift at the release boundary that is an artifact, not the ocean. Nothing errors; the ShortNames differ by one character. So: **one release throughout, named in the report.** The gotcha's prescription for SSH work is V4R4B.

Two more that bind the plan:
- `gotchas/ecco-ssh-ib-variants.md` (stable): SSH is not one field. `SSH` (inverse-barometer corrected), `SSHNOIBC`, `SSHIBC`, `ETAN` are distinct quantities. For a sea level trend the variant is `SSH`, stated.
- `gotchas/ecco-boussinesq-global-steric.md` (stable): the Greatbatch mass-conservation correction bites the *global mean* only. A North Atlantic basin mean is regional, so it does not need the correction — but it is also therefore not a statement about global sea level rise.

## The trend method, whichever region you pick

Two high-severity gotchas govern this and both are already closed by the sanctioned method (`knowledge/computations/ecco-trend-ci.md`, stable):
- `ecco-trend-deseasonalize-jointly.md`: removing the monthly climatology first and fitting second hands the climatology 143/(144Y²−1) of the trend. Over 26 years that's small (0.15%), but a *raw* fit with no climatology carries a seasonal projection instead — worth 0.19 mm/yr, 7%, on the bundle's own 312-month steric example.
- `ecco-trend-without-effective-n.md`: monthly ocean series have lag-1 residual autocorrelation around 0.5–0.9, so a textbook standard error is too small by roughly √((1+r₁)/(1−r₁)). Calibration on the sanctioned code measures 92–95% coverage for the corrected interval at ≥120 months against 47–49% for the naive one at r₁ = 0.8.

So: one joint least-squares fit of trend plus twelve monthly offsets, effective sample size n(1−r₁)/(1+r₁) capped at n, Student's t on n_eff − 2. Never a bare polyfit.
