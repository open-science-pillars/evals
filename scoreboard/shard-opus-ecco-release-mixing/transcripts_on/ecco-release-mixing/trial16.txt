## The collections, and the trap in "whatever covers the full period"

1992–2017 *is* the entire ECCO v4r4 record (`knowledge/podaac/datasets/ecco-v4r4.md`, stable: "Period 1992 through 2017"), so no stitching is needed — every SSH collection spans the whole window. The trap is the opposite of what the phrasing suggests: PO.DAAC ships **two releases** of SSH, and combining them is a high-severity error.

- `ECCO_L4_SSH_LLC0090GRID_MONTHLY_V4R4` — DOI 10.5067/ECL5M-SSH44
- `ECCO_L4_SSH_LLC0090GRID_MONTHLY_V4R4B` — the corrected re-release, DOI 10.5067/ECL5M-SSH4B

Per `gotchas/ecco-release-mixing.md` (high, stable, human-verified): "An SSH or OBP series, trend, or comparison assembled from a mix of V4R4 and V4R4B granules folds the release correction into the geophysical signal: an apparent step or drift at the release boundary is an artifact of the switch, not the ocean." One release throughout, named in the methods.

**There is a live conflict in the bundle here, and I'm not resolving it silently.** That gotcha says SSH work uses the corrected V4R4B. But `validity-domains/ecco-large-scale-statistics.md` scopes its support to `releases: [V4R4]`, and the bundle's own SSH trend finding declares against V4R4. Precedence (stable outranks draft; the validity domain is an unsigned draft) points to **V4R4B** for the headline series — that's my recommendation, and it's a one-flag change. Note the consequence: the sanctioned executors hardcode V4R4, so any cross-check against the attested partition has to be run on V4R4.

Second convention: SSH is not one field. `SSH` (IB-corrected), `SSHNOIBC`, `SSHIBC`, `ETAN` are distinct quantities (`gotchas/ecco-ssh-ib-variants.md`, medium, stable). I defaulted to `SSH`; it must be stated, and it must match the IB convention of any altimetry you later compare against.

## The trend, when you get there

Never a bare `polyfit`. Two high/stable gotchas govern it, and both are closed by one sanctioned tool (`skills/ecco/scripts/ecco_trend_ci.py`, hash pinned by its concept):

- `ecco-trend-without-effective-n.md`: monthly ocean series are serially correlated; OLS's textbook error is too small by ~√((1+r₁)/(1−r₁)), a factor of three at r₁ = 0.8. The bundle's nearby SSH series shows exactly this — r₁ 0.82 collapsing 312 months to ~31 effective samples.
- `ecco-trend-deseasonalize-jointly.md`: removing the climatology first and fitting second hands the climatology 143/(144Y²−1) of the trend. At 26 years that's small (0.15%), but the *raw* fit is the bigger error — on the bundle's record steric series it reads +2.9932 vs +2.7958 mm/yr, ~7% of the trend.

312 months is 26 complete years, so `--deseasonalize climatology` is available and is the right setting — this is the regime the method is calibrated for (91.8–95.1% measured coverage at ≥120 months).

For expectation-setting only: the bundle's `findings/us-northeast-sea-level-rise.md` (**draft, unsigned, verdict UNADJUDICATED**) reports 5.25 mm/yr [4.06, 6.43] over the 35–45°N, 75–65°W box for this period. A basin-wide North Atlantic mean should come out well below that — don't treat it as a target.
