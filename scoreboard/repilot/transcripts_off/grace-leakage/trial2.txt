## The region you named is the documented failure mode for this product

`knowledge/podaac/gotchas/grace-coastal-leakage.md` (status stable, severity high, human-verified 2026-07-04) names your case almost verbatim:

> Nearshore ocean mass series inherit land signal: apparent coastal sea level or ocean-mass trends **near Greenland**, Alaska, or major river basins can be dominated by leakage rather than ocean change. **Nothing errors; the series just is not what its coordinates claim.**

The geometry is the problem. JPL mascons are 3° equal-area spherical caps — order 300 km. The SE Greenland shelf is roughly 100–200 km wide. It is distributed on a 0.5° grid, but per `knowledge/podaac/datasets/grace-fo-mascons.md`, those cells only *represent* the mascons; the native information scale is the mascon. So the shelf contains essentially **no whole ocean mascon** — it sits entirely inside coastline-straddling blocks, and the neighbouring land blocks cover Helheim, Kangerdlugssuaq and Køge Bugt, the highest-ablation sector of the ice sheet. The CRI filter partitions those straddling mascons but, per the same concept, "reduces leakage substantially but does not eliminate it."

A trend computed on that polygon will come back strongly negative and look like real ocean mass loss. It would mostly be ice.

Three more things that bite this specific window, all from the same bundle:

- **Good news on the gap** — 2019–2024 is entirely GRACE-FO, so `grace-intermission-gap` doesn't apply. That trap is avoided by your window.
- **GAD fix, directly on point.** The recipe records that a July 2025 fix "corrected the GAD mass added back to the **ocean part of land/ocean mascons**" — exactly the cells your region is made of — and only files extending past March 2025 carry it. The file version has to be named.
- **Accelerometer transplant.** RL06.3 v4 differs from v3 in the transplant bundle touching the wide-dead-band months: Jan–Feb 2023 and July 2023 onward. That's a third of your window.
