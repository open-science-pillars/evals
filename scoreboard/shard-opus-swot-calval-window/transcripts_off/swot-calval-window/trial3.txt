## The series I'd actually build

Not one series. The honest answer is that **SWOT is the wrong instrument for a 13-month time axis over this region**, and the right build depends on what you want.

**If you want SWOT specifically — a fixed-track series.** Pick one pass number crossing the Agulhas and build SSHA across cycles for that pass alone. Same geometry every revisit, so differences are ocean change rather than sampling change. Cadence 21 days, roughly 8 points from August 2023 to March 2024. This is the cleanest thing SWOT gives you here: a repeated cross-section through the jet or the retroflection.

**The regional-mean-per-pass alternative, and why it's weaker.** Trim each whole-pass granule to the box, flag-mask, apply the crossover correction, area-weighted mean over good pixels, indexed by (cycle, pass, time) — irregular, maybe 25–35 points. The problem: each pass samples a *different part* of the box. In the Agulhas — a narrow meandering jet shedding rings with SSH anomalies of tens of centimetres — the variance from the sampling footprint moving can exceed the variance from the ocean changing. That series conflates the two and shouldn't be read as regional sea level.

**What SWOT is really for here is not a scalar series at all**: a sequence of 2-D swath snapshots of ring edges, filaments and the retroflection front. One caveat worth knowing up front — the full scene is only ~120 km across, and Agulhas rings run 200–300 km, so a pass cuts a *chord* through a ring rather than imaging it whole. You get the submesoscale edge structure beautifully; you don't get the ring.

## What would corrupt it

- **Aliasing.** Agulhas mesoscale evolves on ~10–30 days and rings translate ~5–10 km/day, so a ring moves on the order of its own radius between 21-day revisits. Read the science-orbit points as independent snapshots, not resolved evolution.
- **Seasonal cycle and trend: don't.** Eight science-orbit points is less than one annual cycle. A seasonal harmonic plus a trend cannot be separated from mesoscale variability with that. I'd decline to fit one.
- **Mixed processing baselines.** Thirteen months spans reprocessing, so record the `crid` of every granule loaded and check for a step at any baseline boundary before interpreting a change — a naive multi-cycle series can carry a discontinuity that looks physical.
- **The crossover correction arrives unapplied** (`height_cor_xover`, with a quality gate) — statistics computed without it are not SSHA statistics.
- **Uncertainty understates.** The per-sample uncertainty variable does not cover the cross-track systematic (roll/baseline) error — which is exactly the error that contaminates a regional mean and, far worse, any spatial derivative. If you want geostrophic velocity or relative vorticity from SSH gradients, that second derivative amplifies cross-track systematics badly, and that pushes you to the Expert tier for the full correction and uncertainty set, not Basic.
- **This region is a high-sea-state one.** Southern Ocean swell means large SWH, which is where the sea-state bias correction and KaRIn's SWH-dependent noise floor are at their worst. Expect a higher flagged fraction than in calm water — and **plot the good-pixel fraction as its own series**, because a coverage change masquerades as an SSHA change.
