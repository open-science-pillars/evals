## The time series — general method, not concept-backed

Flagging clearly: the following is standard practice, *not* read from the concepts, and the dataset-specific parts need checking against them once the bundle is back.

**The window you picked straddles the orbit change, and that's the dominant design constraint.** SWOT flew a 1-day repeat cal/val orbit in the first half of 2023 and moved to the 21-day science orbit in the second half; March 2023 is likely before KaRIn science SSH begins at all. The two phases have *different ground tracks*, so there is no single fixed-geometry series spanning March 2023 → March 2024. The orbit-phase gotcha is the concept that settles the actual dates, cycle numbering, and the version-family trap (an empty early-mission query is a family symptom before it's a coverage fact) — I won't quote dates or cycle numbers I can't read.

So the honest answer is **two series, not one**:

- A short, high-cadence segment on the cal/val tracks — daily revisit, but only wherever those few fixed tracks happen to cross your box. Good for sub-weekly evolution on a line; useless as a regional mean.
- A 21-day repeat segment from the science orbit through March 2024 — roughly ten cycles. This is the one worth building.

**Structure of the science-orbit series.** Granules are whole passes, so the regional search returns every pass touching the box and the load trims afterward. I would *not* build a box-mean SSHA time axis first; the box mean mixes different passes covering different sub-regions on different days, and in the Agulhas it's dominated by whichever rings happen to sit under the swath. Better:

1. **Per-pass, per-cycle** as the primitive: fix each pass crossing the box, one sample per cycle at repeating cross-track geometry. Repeating geometry means the correlated cross-track error is near-stationary, so cycle-to-cycle differences are far cleaner than absolute values.
2. Keep the two swaths separate with the nadir gap intact — independent error profiles, and no interpolation across the gap.
3. Decode the bit-packed flags and mask before any statistic; report masked fraction and dominant reasons. The Agulhas is a high-wind, high-wave region, so expect a non-trivial flagged fraction.
4. Apply the crossover correction — it arrives unapplied, and the field name plus its quality gate live in the crossover gotcha.
5. Record the `crid` baseline of every granule loaded. Over 13 months a subset can span baselines, and within-collection baseline drift can put a step into a series that looks geophysical.

**Derived quantities worth more than the SSHA mean.** Cross-swath geostrophic velocity and EKE, SSHA variance per cycle, and ring identification/tracking — these use the 2D swath structure, which is the reason to use KaRIn here instead of nadir altimetry. A box-mean SSHA series throws that away.

**What the series can and cannot support.** Ten science-orbit cycles over eight months: it can characterize mesoscale variability, ring shedding events, and cross-validate against DUACS/nadir. It **cannot** give a trend, and it cannot separate a seasonal cycle from interannual variability — I'd refuse to quote either from this record. Also note SSHA is referenced to a mean sea surface model, and MSS error is large where the mean dynamic topography gradient is steep, which is exactly the Agulhas; that biases the anomaly field in a spatially structured way.

**Uncertainty.** The per-sample uncertainty variables travel with the data, but they characterize random error — they do not cover the correlated cross-track and baseline errors that a repeat-pass series is most exposed to. The dataset concept's Uncertainty section is what states the actual caveat; I'm not going to paraphrase it from memory.
