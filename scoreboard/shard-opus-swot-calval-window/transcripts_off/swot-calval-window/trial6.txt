## The time series I'd build

**The time axis is pass-defined, not calendar-defined.** Granules are whole passes; the Agulhas box is sampled only when a pass crosses it. So the native product is one sample per (cycle, pass) intersection — irregular spacing, and each sample covers a *different footprint* within the box.

That footprint heterogeneity is the trap. A regional-mean SSHA computed per pass mostly measures *which* part of the retroflection that pass happened to cross. In a region with this much mesoscale energy, pass-to-pass differences would be dominated by eddy sampling, not by any regional signal. So I'd build it in two stages:

1. **Per-pass swath level.** For each cycle/pass touching the box: decode the bit-packed quality flags, mask, apply the crossover correction with its quality gate (the field and gate come from `swot-crossover-unapplied.md`), keep left and right swaths separate with the nadir gap intact — never filled. Record valid-pixel count, flagged fraction with dominant reasons, and the granule's `crid`, since baseline drift within a collection means the subset's consistency claim depends on that record.
2. **Bin, then average.** Grid good pixels to fixed cells (≈0.25°), build a per-cell series, then take an area-weighted regional mean over cells that have coverage in *every* period. Averaging into a fixed footprint is what makes the samples comparable; averaging raw passes does not.

**Two regimes, not one series.** The pre- and post-transition segments differ in cadence and in spatial sampling, so I'd plot and report them as two segments rather than concatenating into one evenly spaced record. If you want a single cadence, resample both to a common interval and state plainly that the early segment's footprint is fixed-swath while the later one migrates.

**What the series does *not* support.** Thirteen months is one annual cycle — not enough to fit and remove a seasonal climatology from the record itself, and nowhere near enough for a trend in a region this energetic. I'd refuse to quote a trend from it and would say so in the output rather than compute one quietly.

**Uncertainty.** The per-sample `ssha` uncertainty variables don't cover the correlated cross-track/roll errors, so a regional mean has an error floor well above `σ/√N` and effective DOF far below the pixel count. I'd use a block bootstrap over passes, and cross-check the regional mean against gridded nadir altimetry over the same box.

**A better index, if you're open to it.** Regional-mean SSHA is the weakest thing SWOT can give you here. Its actual advantage over nadir altimetry in the Agulhas is two-dimensional structure: EKE from geostrophic velocities of the SSH gradient field, SSH variance, or retroflection position. Any of those makes a more interpretable 13-month series than a box mean. Say the word and I'll plan that instead.
