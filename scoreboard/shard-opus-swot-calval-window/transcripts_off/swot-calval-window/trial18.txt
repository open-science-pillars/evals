## The time series I'd build

**Two records, not one.** The science-orbit portion is the continuous series; the cal/val portion is a separate high-cadence process-study subset, plotted alongside but never concatenated.

**Per-timestep statistic — repeat-track, not box-mean.** A regional mean SSHA over the Agulhas is a trap: the current core and retroflection carry O(1 m) SSH gradients and energetic rings, so a box average moves according to which part of the box the swath caught. I'd instead difference each pass against its own repeat-track mean, which cancels geoid/MDT error and sampling mismatch, then aggregate per cycle. Per-swath (left/right) summaries kept separate — independent error profiles — and the nadir gap left as a void.

**Corrections and flags, before any statistic.** Bit-packed quality flags decoded and flagged pixels masked; the crossover correction applied via its `height_cor_xover` field with the concept's quality gate. Unapplied, the cross-track systematic error aliases straight into a regional mean and into anything resembling a trend. I'd report masked fraction and dominant flag reasons.

**A discontinuity check on processing baseline.** Baselines (CRIDs) drift within a collection, and a baseline change mid-record shows up as a step. Record which baselines the granules carry and test for breaks at those boundaries *before* interpreting any low-frequency signal.

**What 13 months can and cannot support.** It cannot support a sea level trend — that's one annual cycle, with seasonal and interannual inseparable, and the post-transition segment is shorter still. What it *does* support, and what I'd actually target: mesoscale eddy statistics, eddy kinetic energy from geostrophic velocities off the SSHA gradients (SWOT's real advantage — two-dimensional structure nadir altimetry can't resolve), retroflection position, and ring-shedding events. That's the honest deliverable from this window.

**Uncertainty.** Carry the per-sample SSHA uncertainty variables through, with the dataset concept's caveat about what they don't cover — typically not the cross-track systematic error and not residual geophysical corrections.
