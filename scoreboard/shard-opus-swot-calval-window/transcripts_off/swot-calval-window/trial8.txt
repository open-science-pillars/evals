## The time series I'd build

Assuming the Agulhas box as roughly 10–45°E, 45–25°S — covering the current core along the shelf edge, the retroflection, and the Cape Basin ring corridor. Say if you want it tighter.

**Repeat-track, per pass — not a regional mean.** For each pass number crossing the box, one series over cycles of masked SSHA on that fixed swath geometry. This keeps the sampling footprint constant between samples, which a box-average does not: consecutive passes cut different slivers, so a "regional mean SSHA" series mixes spatial and temporal variability and reads as signal.

**Two series, plotted separately, gap shown as a gap.** Science-orbit passes at 21-day cadence give roughly 17 samples each over the post-transition stretch. Cal/val passes give near-daily sampling of far fewer tracks. Different error profiles, different aliasing, different spatial coverage. Concatenating them manufactures a discontinuity that looks physical.

**The quantity worth the swath.** Per-cycle SSHA variance, or EKE from swath-derived geostrophic velocities, per pass — these are what two-dimensional SSH buys you and what the Agulhas is interesting for. A regional-mean SSHA series is the most error-prone thing you can extract here: it lives at exactly the low wavenumbers where cross-track systematic error and the unapplied crossover correction dominate. If you want basin-mean sea level, nadir altimetry is the better instrument and SWOT is the wrong tool.

**Caveats that would travel with it.** SSHA is already an anomaly against a mean sea surface model, so the series inherits that model's errors and reference epoch. Processing baseline (`crid`) gets recorded per granule — a mid-series baseline change over 13 months is a plausible step. Per-swath summaries with the nadir gap intact, never interpolated. Uncertainty from the product's own per-sample fields, with along-swath correlation respected in any error bar.

**What it can't answer.** Thirteen months is one annual cycle: you cannot separate seasonal from interannual, and there's no trend to extract. Frame it as a description of mesoscale variability and ring-shedding events, not a rate. In the Cape Basin especially, 21-day sampling against ring translation speeds is an aliasing question worth checking before interpreting any peak.
