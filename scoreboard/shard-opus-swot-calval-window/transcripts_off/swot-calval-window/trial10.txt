## The thing that changes this request

Your window straddles SWOT's orbit-phase transition, and that is not a detail — it decides what a "time series" can even mean here.

- **March–~April 2023 is essentially empty.** SWOT was still in commissioning; routine KaRIn L2 SSH doesn't start at the beginning of your window.
- **~April–mid-July 2023 is the 1-day repeat cal/val orbit:** daily revisits, but only over a fixed, sparse set of ground tracks. Whether any of them cross the Agulhas is the first thing to check — if none do, that whole segment is zero granules for your box.
- **A gap during orbit transition** (roughly mid-to-late July 2023).
- **~Late July 2023 → March 2024 is the 21-day science orbit**, global swath coverage, cycles renumbered from 1.
- **The two phases live in different collections / version families.** Querying only the science-orbit ShortName across 2023-03→2024-03 returns nothing for the early months and gives no error. That silent-empty result is the trap to guard against.

So the usable, regularly-sampled record for the Agulhas is roughly **August 2023 – March 2024: about 11–12 cycles.** Anything from the cal/val phase is a *separate* series on different geometry and must never be concatenated into the 21-day one.

## The time series I would build

**The sampling unit is `(cycle, pass, swath side)`.** That's the honest atom. A 21-day repeat with ~120 km swaths means a regional "mean" mixes signal with sampling geometry that changes cycle to cycle. So the design rule is: **fix the geometry, then let time vary.** Build per-pass series, not per-box series.

**1. Agulhas Current surface-transport proxy — the primary series.**
Pick the pass(es) crossing the current obliquely near 32–35°S, upstream of the retroflection. Along a fixed cross-current section on a fixed swath side, take the inshore-minus-offshore SSHA difference → geostrophic surface transport proxy. One value per cycle, ~21-day cadence, ~11–12 points. Because the section is identical every cycle, cycle-to-cycle change is signal, not resampling.

**2. Retroflection EKE.**
Geostrophic velocities from the 2D SSHA gradients (this is the thing nadir altimetry cannot give you), then per-cycle EKE within the box, computed *per swath* and reported alongside the fraction of the box actually sampled that cycle. Without that coverage fraction the series is uninterpretable.

**3. Agulhas ring census.**
Per cycle: count, amplitude, radius of closed SSHA features. Caveat that limits this one — rings are 200–300 km across and the swath is ~120 km wide, so most rings are clipped. This series only works co-registered with gridded DUACS altimetry for ring identification, with SWOT supplying the interior structure.

**4. Small-scale variance and spectral slope — the most defensible SWOT-only series.**
Per cycle, the along-swath SSHA wavenumber spectrum in the 15–150 km band: one variance and one slope per cycle. It's computed entirely within a swath, so it's insensitive to partial box coverage, and the Agulhas is energetic enough that the submesoscale signal sits well above the instrument noise floor.

### What the series can and cannot support

**Roughly 11 points over 8 months.** That is a descriptive, event-resolving record — it can show a retroflection occlusion, a ring shedding, a meander passing a section. It cannot support a trend, and it cannot resolve a seasonal cycle. I would refuse to fit a trend line to it, and I'd say so on the figure.

### Caveats that must travel with every number

- **Crossover calibration arrives unapplied.** `height_cor_xover` must be added, gated on its quality field, before any statistic. Stating explicitly that it was applied is part of the output.
- **Flag masking is seasonally biased.** High sea state in the Southern Ocean in austral winter means more flagged pixels exactly when the region is most energetic — so the masked fraction is itself correlated with the signal. Report masked fraction and dominant flag reasons per cycle.
- **Processing baselines drift within a collection.** Granules reprocessed at different times carry different baselines; a series built across a mixed set can show steps that are pure processing artifact. Record the baseline per granule and report the mix.
- **Mean sea surface error aliases into SSHA in strong currents.** The Agulhas is precisely where the MSS model is worst, and that error projects onto your transport proxy. Worth checking which MSS the chosen `ssha` variant uses.
- **Never interpolate across the nadir gap,** and never grid the swaths as a loading step — if the analysis needs a grid, that's a deliberate, separately-justified step.
- Per-pixel uncertainty variables come along with the data, but they do not cover correlated cross-track error, which is the dominant term for a cross-swath difference like series #1.
