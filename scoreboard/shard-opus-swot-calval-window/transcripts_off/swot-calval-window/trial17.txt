## The window you asked for is not one time series

March 2023 – March 2024 straddles SWOT's orbit change, and a naive concatenation would produce a series whose sampling changes character twice:

- **March 2023** — commissioning. Effectively no science-grade KaRIn SSH.
- **~April to early July 2023** — the **1-day repeat cal/val orbit**. Daily revisit, but only over a small set of fixed swath footprints. Whether the Agulhas is under one of them is a coverage question I can't answer without the archive. Crucially, the cal/val era is held in a *different version family* — so querying the science-era collection over this span returns **empty**, and that empty result reads as "no Agulhas coverage" when it's actually a collection mismatch. This is the single most likely way this request goes silently wrong.
- **~late July 2023 onward** — the **~21-day science orbit**, two ~50 km swaths either side of a ~20 km nadir gap, near-global.

So the revisit interval goes nothing → 1 day → 21 days, over a completely different footprint. Any variance, EKE, or trend statistic computed across those boundaries measures the sampling change, not the ocean.

## What I'd actually build

**Two separate series, plotted separately, never spliced.**

**Primary — science orbit, ~Aug 2023 to Mar 2024 (~11 cycles).** Box roughly 10°E–45°E, 45°S–25°S, covering the Agulhas Current core, the retroflection, the Return Current, and ring corridors into the Cape Basin. Per cycle, over flag-gated pixels only, with the crossover correction applied and stated:

- area-weighted mean SSHA and SSHA variance inside a **fixed common footprint** — the intersection of the box with the pixel mask common to all cycles. Without that, the series inherits footprint wobble rather than ocean signal.
- EKE from geostrophic velocities off along- and cross-swath SSH gradients, kept per-swath (left/right have independent error profiles, and cross-track error grows toward swath edges).
- a ring/eddy count from closed SSHA contours, plus retroflection-front position.
- per-point: valid-pixel fraction, dominant flag reasons, and the processing baselines (`crid`) present.

**Secondary — cal/val, only if an Agulhas footprint exists.** Daily cadence, April–early July 2023, its own record, over whatever narrow strip the 1-day orbit covers.

**What it would show.** The Agulhas retroflection is among the most energetic mesoscale regions in the ocean; the series will be dominated by ring-shedding events (rings of order 200–400 km with several-tenths-of-a-metre SSH anomalies), not by a seasonal cycle. Eleven science-orbit cycles is not enough to resolve an annual cycle, and nowhere near enough for a trend — so I'd frame it as an event/structure record, not a climate series. If you want a genuine continuous 13-month Agulhas SSH record, gridded nadir altimetry (DUACS/CMEMS L4) is the right instrument, with SWOT layered on for the structure it resolves that nadir altimetry cannot.
