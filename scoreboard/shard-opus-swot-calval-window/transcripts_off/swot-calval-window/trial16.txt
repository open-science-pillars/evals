## The time series I'd build (general method, not concept-backed)

**Don't build a box-mean.** The Agulhas is the highest-eddy-energy region in the world ocean outside the Gulf Stream. A regional average of SSHA collapses ±30–50 cm ring and meander signals into a small, noisy residual, and each 21-day sample aliases a field that reorganizes faster than the repeat interval.

**Build a per-pass Hovmöller instead.** Pick one or two passes that cut the Retroflection and the Return Current cleanly, and plot SSHA against along-swath distance versus cycle number, kept separate for the left and right swaths with the nadir gap left empty. This holds the sampling geometry and the cross-track error profile fixed, which a regional mean does not — and it shows ring shedding and westward propagation directly, which is what you actually want from the Agulhas.

Alongside it, one scalar per cycle that survives sparse sampling: **regional SSHA variance or RMS over good pixels** — an EKE-like proxy, far more robust to which passes happened to fall where than the mean is.

Decisions that shape it:

- **Time axis is cycle, not date.** ~21 days per sample in the science era, so roughly 11–13 points from the orbit switch to March 2024. Enough to see ring events; not enough to fit a seasonal cycle and a trend separately, and with mesoscale decorrelation of weeks to months the effective degrees of freedom are in the single digits. **I would not quote a trend from this window.**
- **Crossover correction before any statistic.** The delivered `ssha` arrives with it unapplied; `height_cor_xover` carries it and has a quality gate (`skills/swot/SKILL.md:108`). Uncorrected cross-track error aliases into exactly the low-frequency regional wobble a time series is built to detect — this is the single most likely way to manufacture a fake Agulhas signal.
- **Record `crid` per granule and plot it under the series.** Over 13 months the granules will carry more than one processing baseline, and a step in the series can be a reprocessing step rather than ocean.
- **Flags decoded and pixels masked first; swaths and gap intact.** Never interpolate across the nadir gap — it's a real void.
- **Uncertainty** from the per-sample uncertainty variables the dataset concept names, plus a block bootstrap sized to the mesoscale decorrelation scale, not a naive standard error.
- **Backbone series from nadir altimetry.** Gridded L4 SSH gives you continuous coverage across the entire March 2023 – March 2024 window, including the months where KaRIn science data doesn't exist. Use that as the continuous line and SWOT as the high-resolution overlay on top of it — that's the honest way to cover your stated period.
