## The time series — general method, not concept-backed

Labeling this clearly: the following is general altimetry method plus what the two skill bodies say about the concepts. None of it is a substitute for reading them.

**The shape of the object.** Not a daily gridded series. SWOT samples the box as discrete swath overpasses, so the natural product is **irregular, one record per (cycle, pass) granule trimmed to the box** — and resolved *per swath*, left and right kept separate, because the two carry independent error profiles. Each record: area-weighted mean `ssha` over good pixels, plus valid-pixel count, flagged fraction, and the granule's processing baseline (`crid`). The companion count/flag series are not diagnostics you discard; they are part of the series.

**The dominant problem: your window straddles the orbit transition.** March 2023 → March 2024 spans the mid-2023 cal/val-to-science handover. That changes the sampling cadence, the cycle numbering, *and* the version family in one step — the skill notes the cal/val era lives only in the D family. Consequences:

- This is **two segments, not one series**. Concatenating across the transition produces a record whose sampling geometry changes mid-stream; any trend fitted across the join is partly an artifact of that change.
- Whether the Agulhas box was covered *at all* in the cal/val phase is a coverage question I can't answer without the concept. If an early-2023 search comes back empty, the skill is explicit that this is a **version-family symptom before it is a coverage fact** — the wrong-family query returns nothing and looks like a data gap.
- My recommendation once the concepts are readable: build the science-orbit segment as the primary series, and treat the cal/val segment as a separate, densely-sampled comparison record rather than as the first year of one continuous line.

**What would corrupt the series if skipped:**

- **Crossover correction arrives unapplied.** An uncorrected cross-track tilt and inter-swath offset project directly onto a box mean, and drift in that error aliases into apparent regional sea level change. Must be applied with its quality gate, and the summary must say so explicitly.
- **Baseline drift within a collection.** Record `crid` per granule; a series assembled across baselines can carry steps that look like events.
- **Flag masking is not random here.** The Agulhas is a high sea-state region, and rain/sea-state flagging is seasonally modulated. A mean over good pixels therefore has a *seasonally varying sampling bias* — which is why the flagged-fraction series travels with the SSH series rather than being reported once.
- **The nadir gap stays open.** Never interpolated. Which part of the retroflection the gap falls over varies pass to pass, so the box mean's spatial support changes between records — another reason to carry valid-pixel counts.
- **Aliasing.** Agulhas ring shedding and mesoscale evolution run at timescales comparable to the science-orbit repeat, so a regional-mean series is aliased and shouldn't be read as a resolved eddy signal.

**What I'd actually recommend computing.** For the Agulhas specifically, a box-mean SSH series is the weakest use of KaRIn — it throws away the one thing wide-swath gives you. The stronger targets, from the same loaded granules: SSH variance / geostrophic EKE from the 2D swath fields per overpass, or ring-scale SSH structure tracked across overpasses. The box mean is then a context series, not the result.
