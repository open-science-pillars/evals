## The thing that dominates this window

March 2023 – March 2024 straddles SWOT's orbit-phase transition. The mission flew a 1-day repeat cal/val orbit in the first part of 2023, then moved to the 21-day science orbit in the second half. These are not two chunks of one time series — they are two different sampling instruments:

- **1-day phase:** the swath pattern repeats daily. Superb temporal resolution, but only over the fixed ground tracks that orbit happened to lay down. Whether any of them cross your Agulhas box is a coverage fact I could not check.
- **21-day phase:** near-global, but each point is revisited only once or twice per cycle.

There's also a version-family trap the gotcha concept covers: the cal/val era is not held in the same collection family as the science era. Query one ShortName across the whole 13 months and the early part comes back empty — which reads as "no data over the Agulhas" when it actually means "wrong family."

## The time series I'd build

Given that split, I would **not** build one continuous regional series and describe it as SWOT SSH over 13 months. I'd build:

1. **A per-cycle, per-swath regional mean SSHA** — flag-masked, crossover-corrected, area-weighted, left and right swaths kept separate with the nadir gap intact, and the cal/val and science segments plotted as two separate traces on a shared axis with the transition marked. Cadence is ~daily then ~21-day; that discontinuity is the honest presentation.
2. **The series worth more scientifically: regional SSH variance / eddy kinetic energy per cycle.** The Agulhas is a ring-shedding system, and SWOT's value here is the 2D swath structure, not a basin-mean number a nadir altimeter already gives you better. A variance series resolves ring shedding in a way a mean SSHA doesn't.
3. **Derived, if you want the dynamics:** cross-track SSH gradient across the current core → surface geostrophic velocity, giving a jet-transport proxy; and a retroflection longitude index tracked cycle to cycle.

## Caveats that shape it

- **No trend.** Thirteen months, mesoscale-dominated, two sampling regimes. Any slope you fit is aliased eddy energy, not a sea level trend.
- **Processing baseline drift.** A 13-month span will mix CRIDs within a collection. Record the baseline of every granule loaded; consistency claims across the series depend on that record.
- **Crossover correction arrives unapplied** and has a quality gate. It must be applied before any statistic, and the summary must say so explicitly.
- **Never interpolate the nadir gap** — it's a real void.
