## The problem with this window, before you spend the download

Flagging this as general domain knowledge, **not** concept-backed, because the concept that owns it is missing — verify against CMR before committing:

SWOT's first year was two different missions. A 1-day-repeat cal/val phase (roughly April–July 2023) sampled a small set of fixed ground tracks daily; the 21-day science orbit (from roughly late July 2023) sampled globally but infrequently. **March 2023 predates both** — it is commissioning, and a query starting there plausibly returns nothing, silently.

That means your 13-month window is really three regimes: an empty start, a cal/val phase whose coverage of the Agulhas box is *not guaranteed at all* (it depends on whether a 1-day track crosses it), and ~8 months of science orbit. A single time series concatenated across that is a sampling artifact, not an ocean signal. Confirm the cal/val track geometry over 10–40°E before assuming you have a spring 2023 segment.

## The time series I'd build

**Not** a single region-mean-vs-time line. The honest unit of sampling is the pass.

1. **Per-pass series (the primary product).** For each ground track crossing the box, one series: area-weighted mean SSHA over good pixels within the box, left and right swath kept separate, timestamped at pass mid-time. A fixed ground track means fixed sampling geometry, so change in the series is change in the ocean. Roughly one sample per pass per 21-day cycle in the science phase.
2. **Cycle-level composite** built *from* those, never by pooling passes into one mean — different passes see different parts of a strongly inhomogeneous region, so an unbalanced pool turns coverage changes into fake SSH changes.
3. **Variability series rather than mean series.** The Agulhas signal is mesoscale: within-box SSHA variance, and EKE from cross-track geostrophic velocity, are what SWOT adds over nadir altimetry. Cross-track gradients carry the systematic error KaRIn is known for, so smooth deliberately and report the scale.
4. **A retroflection index** — westernmost extent of a chosen SSH contour, or an eddy census per cycle — if the question is really about retroflection behavior.

**Constraints I'd hold throughout:** decode the bit-packed quality flags and report masked fraction before any statistic; keep the nadir gap as a void and never interpolate across it; treat left and right swaths as independent error profiles; record the processing baseline per granule, since baseline drift within a collection can put a processing step-change in the middle of a series; apply the crossover correction (`skills/swot/SKILL.md:108` names the field as `height_cor_xover`, but its quality gate lives in the missing concept); and carry the ssha uncertainty variables through, with an effective-DOF treatment since swath errors are spatially correlated.

**What I would refuse to report from 13 months:** a trend, and a seasonal cycle separated from interannual variability. One annual cycle cannot do that, and at 21-day repeat the Agulhas eddy field is aliased. If you need continuity across March 2023, SWOT alone won't give it — pair it with nadir altimetry or a gridded L4 for the time axis and use SWOT for the spatial structure.
