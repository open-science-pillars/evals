## The time series I'd build — general method, not dataset fact

Labeled as method because the concepts that would pin the specifics weren't readable.

**It isn't a daily gridded series, and shouldn't be forced into one.** SWOT granules are whole passes. A regional request returns every pass whose swath touches the Agulhas box (I'd propose 10–40°E, 45–30°S, covering the retroflection and return current — worth confirming, since it drives volume). Each pass gives two swaths with a real nadir gap between them. So the natural record is **per-(cycle, pass)**: trim to the box, decode the bit-packed quality flags and mask, apply the crossover correction, then reduce to a regional statistic over surviving pixels — per swath, gap intact, never interpolated across.

That gives an **irregular time axis** whose spacing is the repeat interval, with sample count varying across the box — locations near pass crossovers get hit far more often than locations seen by one pass.

**The mid-window discontinuity is the defining feature.** The mission changes orbit phase in mid-2023, so the repeat interval, the pass set over the Agulhas, and the version family holding the data all change partway through your 13 months. A single continuous series across the whole range silently splices two sampling regimes. I'd either segment at the transition and analyze the halves separately, or — more likely what you want — restrict to the science-phase portion for one homogeneous series and treat the earlier data as a separate, sparser look. Whether the Agulhas box has *any* early coverage is an open question: the fast-repeat phase covers a limited pass set, so an empty result there would be a version-family/coverage artifact, not an absence of ocean.

**Two quantities, not one.** A regional mean SSHA is the obvious series, but in the Agulhas it's the weak one — the signal there is mesoscale. I'd pair it with a per-pass variance or eddy-kinetic-energy proxy over the box, which is what actually tracks ring shedding at the retroflection and is what wide-swath altimetry buys you over nadir. Sampling-count weighting matters for both, since the pass geometry is inhomogeneous across the box.

**Two caveats I'd attach to any number.** Granules across 13 months will very likely span more than one processing baseline, so I'd record the `crid` per granule and check for steps at baseline boundaries before interpreting any low-frequency change. And carry the per-sample uncertainty variables through, noting they don't cover correlated cross-track error.

**What it can't answer:** 13 months is one annual cycle. There is no sea level trend in this series, and no way to separate seasonal from interannual. It's a mesoscale variability record. If a trend is the goal, this window and this instrument are the wrong tool and I'd say so before loading anything.
