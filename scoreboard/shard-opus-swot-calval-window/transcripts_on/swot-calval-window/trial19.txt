## The problem with your window

March 2023 – March 2024 is close to the worst possible span to ask for as a single time series, for a reason that has nothing to do with the Agulhas:

- SWOT launched in December 2022 and spent the early part of 2023 in commissioning. **A series starting 1 March 2023 probably starts before there is science-quality KaRIn L2 SSH at all.**
- The mission then flew a **1-day repeat cal/val orbit** through roughly the first half of 2023, and transitioned to the **21-day science orbit** around mid-2023. These are *different ground tracks*, different cycle numbering, and different revisit cadence. Your window straddles that transition.
- The cal/val orbit repeated a limited set of passes, not global coverage. **Whether the Agulhas box sits under a cal/val swath at all is an empirical question** I'd have to answer from granules — I can't assume it either way.
- The two eras are typically held in different product version families. So an empty result for early 2023 is a *wrong-collection* symptom at least as often as a true coverage gap, and the orbit-phase concept is what disambiguates that.

Net: you do not get a 13-month series. You realistically get **~8 months of homogeneous 21-day science sampling** (mid-2023 → March 2024, on the order of a dozen cycles), plus an earlier, differently-sampled chunk that must not be concatenated onto it. Under one full annual cycle of consistent sampling — so no seasonal cycle, and certainly no trend.

## What the time series actually looks like

The unit of acquisition is **cycle × pass**, not a date. Each pass gives an instantaneous snapshot of a two-swath strip with a nadir gap down the middle. For an Agulhas box (I'd assume ~10–40°E, 45–30°S; extend east to ~50°E if you want the Agulhas Return Current — tell me which, it changes the download), only a handful of passes cross it per cycle, each at a different moment within the cycle.

So the honest construction is **one series per pass ID**, indexed by cycle — not a single merged regional series. A box-mean built from whichever swaths happened to be in view on a given day is averaging different pieces of ocean each time; in a region with gradients as sharp as the Agulhas, that sampling noise will dominate whatever signal you're after.

Quantities worth putting on that axis, roughly in order of robustness:

1. **Per-pass SSHA variance / RMS and surface geostrophic EKE** from along-swath gradients. Least exposed to the large-scale correlated swath errors, and it's the Agulhas-relevant quantity — ring shedding and retroflection variability.
2. **Front position** — where a chosen SSHA contour crosses a fixed pass. A clean scalar per cycle, immune to most swath-mean error.
3. **Wavenumber spectra per pass segment** — the submesoscale content SWOT exists to resolve, which nadir altimetry cannot give you here. Report the noise floor alongside.
4. **Swath-mean SSHA** — list it last deliberately. It's the statistic *most* exposed to cross-track correlated (roll/phase) error, which is what the crossover correction addresses, and that correction arrives unapplied in at least some tiers. Don't quote it before applying the correction with its quality gate.

Ring tracking is tempting here and I'd push back on it: 21-day revisit is too coarse to follow an Agulhas ring continuously. You'd use SWOT for 2D structure at snapshots and a merged nadir product (DUACS or similar) as the continuous backbone.

## Other things that will bite

- **SSHA is an anomaly against a mean sea surface.** In the Agulhas — sharp persistent gradients, historically sparse coverage — MSS error is not negligible and projects straight into any regional mean.
- **Processing baseline drift.** Over 13 months the granules will not all carry the same `crid`. Record it per granule; consistency claims depend on that record.
- **Flagged fraction will be substantial**, especially austral winter. Flags are bit-packed and must be decoded before any statistic — no exceptions, and I wouldn't report a number computed any other way.
- **Never interpolate across the nadir gap or flatten the swaths to a grid** as part of loading. That's analysis, done deliberately and separately.
