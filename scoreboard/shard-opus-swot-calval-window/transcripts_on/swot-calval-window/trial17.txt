## The time series I would build

Not one series. Two, and they are not commensurable.

**Science phase (2023-07-21 → 2024-03-31), the one worth building.** The 21-day repeat makes the cycle the natural sample: **one value per cycle, per swath side**, about **12 points** over your window. Concretely:

- Variable: `ssha_karin` **plus `height_cor_xover`**, gated by `height_cor_xover_qual`. This is not optional. The crossover calibration arrives unapplied, and the raw field carries a cross-track ramp spanning ±2.9 m, against corrected mesoscale structure of std 0.17 m. In the Agulhas, where the signal you want *is* the mesoscale, the uncorrected ramp is more than an order of magnitude larger than the signal — an "as-is" region mean would be a confident wrong answer.
- Gating: decode `ssha_karin_qual` bitwise before any statistic; land-gate; check `rain_flag` and `distance_to_coast` over the shelf. Expect roughly 39% valid pixels — that is normal, not a defect.
- Geometry: left and right swaths kept separate with the nadir gap intact. They have independent error profiles, and the gap is a real void. No gridding, no gap-filling at load.
- Subsetting: CMR matches whole pole-to-pole passes, so a matched granule can hold zero in-box pixels. Trim to the box *before* aggregating.
- Carried with every point: valid-pixel fraction, dominant flag reasons, the in-box footprint actually sampled, and each granule's `crid`.

**Cal/val phase (2023-03-30 → 2023-07-10), conditional.** If the 1-day tracks cross the Agulhas, this is a near-daily SSH series at fixed locations — genuinely good for sub-monthly behaviour (Natal pulses, ring shedding at the retroflection) that the 21-day orbit cannot see. But it is point-like, not regional, and it stands as its own product with its own plot.

**What this series can and cannot support.** Twelve samples at 21-day spacing over eight months: it resolves the seasonal march and mesoscale field structure, and it aliases everything faster than ~42 days. It supports **no trend** — a 13-month window with a 12-month gap in the middle of the sampling regime is not a trend record. If a multi-year Agulhas sea level trend is the goal, that is nadir altimetry (the bundle has a `nasa-ssh.md` dataset concept, status draft); SWOT buys you two-dimensional structure, not record length.

Two traps I would build the series to avoid:

- **Footprint drift.** After flag masking the in-box coverage changes cycle to cycle, so a naive "region mean" silently changes its own footprint. Area-weight and report the footprint per point, or the sampling change reads as ocean signal.
- **Baseline drift.** D-family CRIDs run PGD0 through forward-processing baselines across this window. A step in the series is a processing change until you have ruled it out from the recorded `crid` values.

**Uncertainty.** `ssh_karin_uncert` is per-sample *random* noise only. It excludes the correlated swath-scale systematics (roll, phase, timing, wet troposphere residuals) that dominate exactly the swath-scale average this series is made of. The error bar on a per-cycle region mean is therefore **not** `uncert/√N` — that would understate it badly. If you want the full correction and uncertainty stack for a proper error decomposition, that is the Expert tier.
