## The time series I'd build

Not one continuous curve. **Two segments, plotted separately, with no trend fit across the seam** — the sampling density changes by more than an order of magnitude at the transition, so a single line through both is a sampling artifact dressed as a signal.

Within each segment:

- **Pass is the unit, not day.** Granules are whole passes; search the box (~10–45°E, 25–45°S — confirm), then trim to the region *before* aggregating.
- **Per-pass aggregates over good pixels only**: area-weighted mean SSHA, plus the statistics that actually matter for the Agulhas — SSHA variance/RMS as an eddy-energy proxy, and valid-pixel fraction as the honesty column. Left and right swath kept separate; they have independent error profiles.
- **Flags decoded and pixels masked before any statistic**, with masked fraction and dominant flag reasons reported.
- **Crossover correction applied, and said so.** It arrives unapplied in the KaRIn SSH products and carries its own quality gate. In a retroflection region, uncorrected cross-track/roll error aliases straight into the mesoscale structure you're trying to measure — it's the difference between an eddy and an artifact.
- **Nadir gap intact, swaths never flattened** at load. Gridding is an analysis decision made deliberately, later.
- **`crid` recorded per granule.** A 13-month span can mix processing baselines within one collection, so consistency claims downstream depend on that record.
- **Sampling caveat carried with the series:** at science-orbit revisit, a mid-latitude box gets a couple of samples per 21-day cycle, while Agulhas rings and eddies evolve on 10–30 day scales. The series is irregularly spaced and aliased; trends and spectra need methods that know that.
