## The time series I'd build — two, not one

This is the honest answer to your question: you get **two records with different sampling**, and concatenating them into a single 13-month series would alias the orbit change into the signal.

**Cal/val series (Mar 30 – Jul 10 2023).** Near-daily repeat over a fixed, sparse track set. The unit is a pass. This resolves *fast* evolution — eddy translation, ring shedding, sub-weekly mesoscale change — at the specific places tracks cross the box, and nowhere else. It cannot give you a regional mean, because the sampling is a handful of fixed lines, not the region.

**Science series (Jul 21 2023 – Mar 2024).** 21-day repeat, global. The unit is a cycle: roughly 11–12 points across the window. This gives you a genuine box-mean SSHA at 21-day cadence, and per-cycle 2-D maps of the retroflection — but it cannot see anything faster than the repeat interval.

For each series, the per-time-step quantity would be the area-weighted mean of **crossover-corrected** `ssha_karin` over good pixels, computed **per swath** (left/right treated as independent error profiles), with the valid-pixel fraction reported alongside every point.

## Non-negotiables that shape the numbers

- **Add `height_cor_xover`, gated by `height_cor_xover_qual`, before any aggregation.** The crossover calibration arrives unapplied. Uncorrected `ssha_karin` carries a spurious cross-track ramp spanning ±2.9 m; the same scene corrected shows std 0.17 m. Flag gating alone does not make an average safe — the uncorrected field is plausible, populated, and wrong.
- **Subset before aggregating.** CMR spatial search matches whole pole-to-pole passes, so some matched granules carry zero in-box pixels.
- **Don't quote `ssh_karin_uncert` as the error bar on a swath average.** It's per-sample random noise (1σ) and excludes the correlated swath-scale systematics — roll, phase, timing, wet-troposphere residuals — that dominate at long cross-track wavelengths.
- **Nadir gap stays open** (~20 km between two 50 km swaths, 2 km posting). No interpolation across it at load time.
- **Expect heavy masking.** ~39% valid after flag and land gating is normal for an open-ocean pass, not a defect. The Agulhas is rough water; I'd expect the rain and surface-classification flags to bite harder than that.
- **Capture the `crid` attribute per granule.** Baselines drift within a collection as forward processing and reprocessing interleave; the loaded granules' `crid` values are the only record of which baseline a subset came from. Your window likely spans PGD0 into later CRIDs.
