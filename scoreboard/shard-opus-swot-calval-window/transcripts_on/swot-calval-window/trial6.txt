## The time series I would build — general method, not concept-sourced

Labeling this clearly: the following is standard swath-altimetry practice, not read from the missing concepts. Verify every product-specific choice against them before running it.

**Time axis.** Not calendar-regular. Granules are whole passes, so the sampling unit is a pass overflight of the box. Two defensible constructions, and they answer different questions:

1. **Per-pass series** — one series per pass number, each sampled at that pass's repeat interval, trimmed to a fixed in-box footprint. Sampling geometry is constant within a series, so differences are ocean, not coverage. This is the one I'd build.
2. **Per-cycle regional aggregate** — one value per cycle, pooling all passes crossing the box. Denser, but each sample mixes passes acquired days apart over different sub-regions, so coverage changes alias into the signal. Only useful with an explicit constant-coverage mask.

**Segmentation.** At minimum two segments, split at the phase transition, never concatenated into one curve: the sampling cadence, the pass set, the cycle numbering, and the version family all change across it. Plot them as separate panels sharing a y-axis. Whether the March 2023 portion yields any science SSH at all is precisely what the orbit-phase concept answers — treat it as an open question, not an assumed gap.

**Per-swath, always.** Left and right swaths carry independent cross-track error profiles; summarize them separately and never interpolate across the nadir gap — it's a real void, and filling it fabricates SSH.

**Quantities worth extracting,** in rough order of what SWOT adds over nadir altimetry:

- **Cross-track SSH slope → geostrophic surface velocity**, resolved in two dimensions across the swath. This is the point of KaRIn in a boundary-current system, and the Agulhas jet is well above the noise floor.
- **In-box SSHA variance / mesoscale energy per pass** — a scalar per sample, robust to partial coverage if the mask is fixed.
- **Area-averaged SSHA over a fixed, fully-covered sub-box.** Only over a footprint every sample covers; averaging a varying footprint gives you a different quantity each time.
- **Ring and retroflection structure** as a sequence of snapshots, not a track. At a 21-day repeat, rings translating at several cm/s move on the order of a hundred kilometres between samples — you get well-resolved stills, not continuous trajectories. Continuous tracking is a 1-day-phase capability, and only over that phase's fixed pass set.

**What this series will not support.** About 17 samples per pass over 13 months, split across a phase change. That is a mesoscale snapshot record. It will not give you a seasonal cycle, and it will not give you a trend — any rate fitted to it would be dominated by Agulhas eddy variability, which is the largest in the global ocean. If a trend or seasonal cycle is the actual goal, SWOT is the wrong instrument for this window; the multi-mission gridded altimetry record is, with SWOT layered on for structure.

**Two further cautions.** SSHA is referenced to a mean sea surface, and the MSS is itself least certain exactly where variability is highest — so "anomaly" carries a region-dependent reference error here. And the Southern Ocean storm track crosses this box, so expect a non-trivial flagged fraction; report it with the dominant flag reasons rather than silently dropping pixels.
