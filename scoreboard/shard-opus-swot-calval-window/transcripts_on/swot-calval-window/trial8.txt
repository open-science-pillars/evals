## The part that actually decides this request

March 2023 → March 2024 straddles SWOT's change of orbit phase. That's the single fact that determines whether your time series is one series or two, and the plugin flags it as a *silent* failure mode in both directions: an empty early-2023 result is a version-family symptom before it's a coverage fact, and a window spanning the transition looks fine while mixing two sampling regimes. The phase dates, the cycle numbering, and which version family holds the cal/val era are dataset facts I'm not willing to supply from memory — that's exactly what the concept exists to prevent. So the collection choice and the early-window plan are genuinely blocked, not just unverified.

## The time series, at the level I can describe honestly

**Structure.** Not a gridded monthly field — a swath-sampled, irregular series. Granules are whole passes, so the Agulhas box (roughly 10–40°E, 45–25°S) is a *trim* applied after a spatial search, before any aggregation. The natural sample is one value per pass-crossing-box, several per repeat cycle at uneven within-cycle spacing, kept separately for the left and right swaths with the nadir gap intact.

**Per-sample recipe.** Decode the bit-packed quality flags and mask before any statistic; apply the crossover correction (the gotcha names the field and its quality gate) and say so explicitly in the output; then an area-weighted mean SSHA over surviving pixels, carrying the product's per-sample uncertainty variables.

**Three things that will bite this particular window:**

1. **A step at the phase transition.** Revisit frequency, and possibly whether the Agulhas box is sampled at all, changes across it. Any discontinuity there is a sampling artifact until proven otherwise — don't fit one trend across the join.
2. **Processing-baseline drift within the collection.** Thirteen months will likely mix CRIDs. Record the baseline of every granule loaded; a baseline change can alias into the series as a fake shift.
3. **Correlated errors along-swath.** Cross-track systematic error means the effective sample count for a regional mean is far below the pixel count, so naive standard errors will be badly optimistic.

**Agulhas-specific.** A box-mean SSHA here is dominated by retroflection ring shedding — you'd largely be measuring how many rings happened to be inside the box, which is noisy and hard to interpret. SSHA variance or an eddy-tracking metric over the swath is usually the more meaningful series, and it's the kind of statistic where the crossover correction matters most.
