## The time series you'd actually get

**Not a scalar per date.** SWOT gives you a swath of 2 km pixels per overpass, and the region is covered by several different passes at different times within each cycle. The series is an **irregularly-sampled set of (cycle, pass) snapshots**, and the overpass times are not evenly spaced.

The defensible construction is **per-pass, at fixed swath coordinates** — for each pass number crossing the box, a series across cycles at the same `(num_lines, num_pixels)`. That compares like with like: same ground location, same cross-track position, therefore the same error profile. A region-mean over whatever swath happened to fall inside the box is dominated by sampling geometry, not ocean.

- **Cal/val phase:** ~100 daily samples over 3.5 months on 1–2 fixed tracks. This is the scientifically interesting half — daily revisit resolves Natal Pulses, meander growth, and ring-shedding, which are ~4–10 day processes. Nothing else observes them this way.
- **Science phase:** ~12 samples per location from late July 2023 to March 2024. **21-day repeat undersamples the Agulhas mesoscale.** Rings and meanders evolve on 10–30 days, so you cannot track an individual eddy through this series from SWOT alone. You get snapshots of two-dimensional structure, not evolution.

Better scalars than a regional mean, computed per pass: cross-track SSH gradient → surface geostrophic velocity where a pass crosses the jet (core speed, jet position, a transport proxy); SSHA variance / EKE inside the box; closed-contour ring counts.

## What shapes the series, beyond the sampling

- **The crossover correction arrives unapplied** (`knowledge/podaac/gotchas/swot-crossover-unapplied.md`, stable, severity high, human-verified). Add `height_cor_xover`, gated by `height_cor_xover_qual`, to `ssha_karin` before any aggregation. Uncorrected, the cross-track roll/phase ramp spans about ±2.9 m against a mesoscale signal of std ~0.17 m — it would swamp every quantity above, and it hits gradients and swath-means hardest. Flag gating alone does not make the numbers safe.
- **`ssh_karin_uncert` is random noise only.** It excludes the correlated swath-scale systematics — roll, phase, timing, wet-troposphere residuals — and those are precisely the errors that project onto a swath-mean or a cross-track gradient. Error bars from `*_uncert` alone understate any series built here.
- **Baseline drift.** CRIDs change across the window as forward and reprocessing interleave. A step at a CRID boundary is processing, not ocean. Record `crid` per granule at load; it's the only record of which baseline a subset came from.
- **Varying valid fraction.** ~39% valid after flag and land gating is normal on an open-ocean pass. The Agulhas will run worse — rain flagging over the convergence zone, high sea state in the retroflection. If valid fraction varies pass to pass, a region-mean is computed over a changing footprint and you get spurious variability. Impose a minimum valid fraction and a common footprint mask.
- **The ~20 km nadir gap stays open.** It's a real void down the centre of every pass; interpolating across it fabricates SSH.
- **No seasonal cycle from this.** Eight months of science orbit can't resolve an annual cycle or separate seasonal from mesoscale, and there is no SWOT climatology to take anomalies against.

## Recommendation

For a continuous 13-month Agulhas record, SWOT is the overlay, not the backbone. Use merged gridded altimetry (DUACS/CMEMS or the MEaSUREs L4) for the continuous series, and bring SWOT in for the two-dimensional structure it uniquely resolves — with the cal/val window treated as a separate, high-cadence process study. The SWOT nadir altimeter (`SWOT_L2_NALT_GDR_D`) is a third option: conventional 1-D altimetry running continuously through both phases.
