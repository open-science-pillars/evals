## The time series I would build

Granules are whole passes, so the spatial search returns every pass touching the box and the load trims to the region **before** any aggregation.

**The unit is the cycle-pass, not the day.** Each pass crossing the box yields one timestamped sample at pass mid-time, computed per swath (left and right kept separate — independent error profiles), over flag-gated pixels only, with the crossover correction applied and stated as applied. The nadir gap stays open; it is a real void.

That gives an **irregularly sampled, two-segment series**, not a monthly one. I'd carry four layers:

1. **Box-mean SSHA per pass** — a sanity baseline, dominated by the large-scale steric seasonal cycle. Coverage-weighted, because the fraction of the box a pass actually sees varies pass to pass with the gap and the flag mask.
2. **SSHA variance / RMS per pass** — the mesoscale eddy-energy index. In the Agulhas this is the signal: ring shedding at the retroflection drives large, intermittent excursions.
3. **Surface geostrophic velocity, relative vorticity and strain** from 2D SSHA gradients — this is what SWOT buys you over nadir altimetry, which cannot form cross-track gradients at all. Per swath, gradients never crossed over the gap.
4. **Per-swath wavenumber spectra** — effective resolution and submesoscale content, and whether that content varies seasonally.

**Three caveats that must ride with every number:**

- **No trend.** Thirteen months cannot separate an annual cycle from a secular trend. I would refuse to quote a regional SSH trend from this window, and a deseasonalized series over it is not meaningful either.
- **The two segments are not one series.** Different repeat period, different pass sets over the box. Plotted together, they get a visible break and separate statistics — never silently concatenated and resampled to a common cadence.
- **Baseline heterogeneity.** Over 13 months a subset will carry mixed processing baselines. I'd record the `crid` of every granule loaded; the dataset concept's known issues explain why consistency claims across the window depend on that record.

Per-pass uncertainty comes from the product's own uncertainty variables, with the caveat the dataset concept records about what the per-sample field does *not* cover.
