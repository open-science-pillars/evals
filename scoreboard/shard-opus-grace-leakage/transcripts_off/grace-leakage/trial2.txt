## The scientific problem with the request as posed

Shelf waters around southeast Greenland is close to the worst-case geometry for a mascon ocean-mass trend:

- JPL mascons are 3° blocks with an **effective resolution of a few hundred km**. "Shelf waters" off SE Greenland is a strip narrower than that — it is not resolved, it is a partition of cells that also contain ice sheet.
- Those cells sit against **the largest land mass-loss signal in the Northern Hemisphere**. Greenland's ice loss is orders of magnitude larger than the ocean-mass signal you are asking for, so even a small fractional leakage across the coast dominates the result. A trend computed there is mostly an artifact of how the land/ocean split was done, not ocean mass.
- The CRI filter partitions land and ocean within coastal mascons, which is exactly what makes the product usable near coasts — but the residual is first-order here, and the `slb_mass_mascons.py` loader in the ocean-science package is explicit that the CRI partition is the product's own and **no further coastal buffer is applied**. It computes a *global* ocean mean for that reason.
- Separately, GRACE trends over 2019–2024 carry a **GIA-model sensitivity** that for some regions exceeds measurement noise, and a 6-year window in a region with strong dynamic variability needs an internal-variability caveat before anything is called a trend.

A single mm/yr number for that strip would be reportable-looking and wrong. I'm not going to produce one.
