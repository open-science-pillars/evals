## Why I'd push back on the region even with data in hand

Southeast Greenland shelf is close to the worst case you can pick for a GRACE mascon product, for reasons that don't go away with careful processing:

- **The target is smaller than the resolution element.** GRACE-FO's native resolution is ~300 km half-wavelength, and JPL mascons are 3° equal-area caps of comparable scale. The SE Greenland shelf is a strip roughly 50–200 km wide between coast and shelf break. No mascon is purely shelf water, so there is no cell that answers the question as posed.
- **The adjacent land signal is one to two orders of magnitude larger than the target signal.** SE Greenland is among the highest mass-loss drainage sectors on the ice sheet, with trends of order tens of cm/yr equivalent water height. The ocean mass signal you're after is a few mm/yr. A small percentage of leakage across the coastline dominates the result outright.
- **The CRI filter's land/ocean partition becomes the answer.** JPL's Coastline Resolution Improvement separates land and ocean contributions within coastal mascons using a prior assumption. Against a mass gradient this extreme, that prior — not the gravity data — is doing most of the work in setting the shelf value.
- **Near-field sea level physics runs opposite to the global signal.** Through self-attraction and loading, Greenland mass loss makes sea level *fall* within roughly 2000 km of the ice sheet. The SE shelf sits inside that fall zone, so the recovered trend is a competition between near-field fall, global mass rise, and leakage — meaning even the sign isn't robust.
- **Six years is short for a regional trend.** 2019–2024 is clean in one respect: it's entirely within GRACE-FO, so no inter-mission gap bridging is needed. But subpolar North Atlantic bottom pressure has strong NAO-driven interannual variability, and once autocorrelation is accounted for the confidence interval on a 6-year regional trend is wide. It would not support a statement about forced change.

There's also a bookkeeping choice that materially moves the answer: over ocean, GRACE gives bottom pressure, and whether the atmospheric/oceanic dealiasing product is restored determines whether you have full OBP or a residual.

## What I'd suggest instead

Three options, depending on what's actually driving the question:

1. **Enlarge the domain** to something GRACE resolves — the Irminger Sea or the wider subpolar North Atlantic, several mascons across and set back from the coast. A mass trend there is defensible with a real uncertainty statement.
2. **If the shelf specifically matters,** GRACE is the wrong instrument. Bottom pressure recorders, or a regional reanalysis, are better suited. Altimetry is also compromised there by seasonal sea ice.
3. **If the underlying interest is Greenland freshwater input to the shelf,** then the SE Greenland drainage-basin *land* mass loss is an excellent GRACE-FO target and is computable with this product.
