### Why I'd push back on the analysis as specified

Southeast Greenland shelf waters are the one place this product structurally cannot give you an ocean mass trend.

**The resolution is not what the grid suggests.** The JPL mascon solution is distributed on a 0.5° grid, but the independent elements are ~3° equal-area mascons, roughly 300 km across. The 0.5° grid is a resampling, not resolution. "Shelf waters around southeast Greenland" is a strip a few tens of km wide in places — well inside a single mascon. You would not be resolving the shelf; you'd be reading a handful of mascons that straddle the coastline.

**Those mascons are dominated by the ice sheet.** Greenland has been losing roughly 250 Gt/yr over this era, and the southeast (the Helheim/Kangerdlugssuaq sector) is one of the largest regional contributors. A coastal mascon sees that. Any trend you extract over the adjacent shelf will be substantially leakage from land ice, carrying a large negative sign that has nothing to do with ocean mass. The real manometric signal you're after in the subpolar North Atlantic is order a few mm/yr of equivalent water — the leakage term is far larger. The signal-to-contamination ratio is not marginal here; it's inverted.

**CRI reduces this; it does not solve it.** The Coastal Resolution Improvement filter partitions land and ocean signal within coastline-straddling mascons, but it does so under an assumption of uniformity within each portion, informed by a prior. Its residual error scales with the magnitude of the land trend being partitioned away. Next to the highest-rate ice loss in the Northern Hemisphere, that residual is first-order, not a correction term.

**And even if leakage were solved, the trend itself is fragile:**

- Six years is short. Subpolar North Atlantic bottom pressure has large NAO-driven wind-forced interannual redistribution; a 2019–2024 regional slope is not a climate trend, and an autocorrelation-aware interval (Sen slope with effective DOF) would very likely span zero.
- GRACE-FO has missing monthly solutions in this window — the sampling is irregular and the endpoints have leverage.
- Regional ocean mass trends are materially sensitive to the degree-1/geocenter and C20/C30 replacement choices.
- The GIA correction baked into the product is model-dependent, and Greenland is exactly where GIA and present-day elastic loading gradients are strongest.
- You'd need to state whether you want ocean bottom pressure with GAD restored or the dealiased "ocean mass" field — they are different numbers.
