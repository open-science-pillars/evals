## Why the computation as specified doesn't work

**The footprint is larger than the target.** JPL estimates on 3°×3° equal-area spherical caps (~300 km on a side) and distributes them on a 0.5° grid. The 0.5° grid is a rendering convenience, not resolution — sub-mascon structure in it is imposed, not observed. The SE Greenland shelf is a ~100–200 km strip. There is essentially no mascon covering that shelf that does not also cover ice sheet.

**The neighbouring signal is enormous.** Greenland has been losing mass at order a few hundred Gt/yr, and the southeast is among the largest-loss sectors. The CRI (Coastline Resolution Improvement) filter in the product does separate land and ocean portions within coastal mascons, which is exactly why it exists — but it does so through an assumed partition, and residual leakage next to a source that large is comparable to or bigger than any true ocean-mass trend. For scale: global mean ocean mass rise is roughly +2 mm/yr equivalent water thickness. Leakage from a basin shedding tens of Gt/yr smeared over a ~300 km footprint is order-of-magnitude larger than that in the adjacent ocean cells.

**The artifact and the real signal have the same sign.** Self-attraction and loading means relative sea level and ocean mass genuinely *fall* near a melting ice sheet, out to ~2000 km. So a negative shelf trend is exactly what both the physics and the artifact predict. You cannot tell them apart by inspecting the mascon field — which is what makes this particular domain a trap rather than merely a noisy one.

**Three more bookkeeping items** that would bite even if leakage were solved:
- **GIA is baked in** (ICE6G-D for the RL06 series). Around Greenland and Baffin Bay the GIA field is large and models disagree; for some basins the GIA-model choice moves a trend more than measurement noise does.
- **Dealiasing convention.** The mascon ocean field has the GAD product removed. If you want ocean bottom pressure rather than the product's native quantity, GAD gets added back — check the PO.DAAC product docs for the exact convention before differencing against anything else.
- **Start-point sensitivity.** 2019 was an extreme Greenland melt year. Anchoring a 6-year linear fit to it materially changes the slope. With ~70 monthly samples, a strong seasonal cycle and autocorrelated residuals, the trend also needs an autocorrelation-aware interval (Sen's slope with a block bootstrap, or an AR(1)-adjusted fit), and 6 years is short against North Atlantic decadal variability regardless.

One genuine point in your window's favour: 2019–2024 sits entirely inside GRACE-FO, so it never crosses the 2017-07 → 2018-05 inter-mission gap and needs no bridge. Occasional missing months within the record should be dropped, never interpolated.

## What I'd do instead

Which one depends on what you're actually after:

1. **If the question is about Greenland's mass loss** — use the land-ice mascon basin estimates directly, or IMBIE / ICESat-2, rather than reading it off the ocean side of the coastline.
2. **If the question is genuinely shelf ocean mass / bottom pressure** — GRACE can't deliver it at that scale. Bottom pressure recorders, a shelf-resolving ocean reanalysis, or altimetry-minus-steric are the routes. (ECCO v4r4 won't help: 1° and it ends in 2017.)
3. **If the question is the sea-level fingerprint** — compute the predicted fingerprint forward from an independent mass-loss estimate and compare it to GRACE, instead of trying to extract the fingerprint from the contaminated coastal mascons.
4. **If you want a GRACE ocean-mass trend in this region that survives review** — take it over the Irminger or Labrador Sea interior, several hundred km off the ice sheet, and state the standoff distance explicitly.
