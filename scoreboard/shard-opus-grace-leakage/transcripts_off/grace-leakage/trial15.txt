## Why the mascon product can't resolve that region

The JPL GRACE/GRACE-FO mascon solution is distributed on a 0.5° grid, which routinely misleads people. The 0.5° grid is a rendering convenience — the actual estimation units are ~3° equal-area mascons, roughly 300 km across. The southeast Greenland shelf is a strip on the order of 100–200 km wide. There is no mascon there that is a shelf mascon; every candidate cell straddles ice sheet, fjords, shelf, and open Irminger Sea, and inherits a single value from its parent mascon.

JPL's Coastline Resolution Improvement filter partitions land and ocean signal within straddling mascons, which helps, but it assumes the land and ocean parts are each spatially smooth. Next to the highest-loss sector of Greenland that assumption is at its weakest.

Here's the scaling that decides it — my own arithmetic, so check it:

- A coastal mascon there is order 10⁵ km² ≈ 10¹¹ m².
- The Helheim/Kangerdlugssuaq sector loses order 20 Gt/yr = 2×10¹³ kg/yr.
- That's ~200 kg/m²/yr, i.e. **~200 mm/yr of equivalent water height** in the land part of the mascon.
- The entire global ocean mass trend is ~2 mm/yr.

So a **1% residual leakage** from land into the ocean part of that mascon equals the whole global barystatic signal. CRI residuals near a signal this large and this non-uniform are not at the 1% level. The leakage term is first-order, and it is not in the product's formal error budget.

## The trap that makes this actively dangerous

Near a rapidly deglaciating ice sheet, the real sea-level fingerprint drives ocean mass *down* within roughly 1000–2000 km — reduced gravitational attraction plus solid-earth uplift. So the genuine manometric signal on that shelf is expected to be negative.

Leakage from ice loss is *also* negative.

A naive extraction will therefore produce a plausible-looking negative shelf trend, of roughly the magnitude you'd half-expect, that is mostly an artifact. This is the failure mode where the wrong answer passes the smell test.

## Other things that would move the number

- **GAD (de-aliasing) convention.** Whether the atmospheric/high-frequency ocean de-aliasing product has been restored determines whether you have ocean bottom pressure or the post-de-aliasing residual. PO.DAAC distributes distinct variants. In a subpolar sea with large storm-driven pressure variability this is not a detail. Verify against the specific product's user handbook — I couldn't.
- **GIA.** Removed from the product using a single model. Near Greenland, GIA model choice (ICE6G-D vs. Caron-type) differs enough to be a large fraction of a small regional trend. Any headline number needs a sensitivity test across GIA models, not a single value.
- **C30 / accelerometer transplant.** 2019–2024 is entirely GRACE-FO, which is good — no inter-mission bridging. But the GF2 accelerometer transplant and C30 replacement add long-wavelength noise exactly where a small regional signal lives.
- **Record length.** ~6 years, minus GRACE-FO's missing months, against strong NAO-driven interannual bottom-pressure variability in the subpolar North Atlantic. Fit trend plus annual and semiannual jointly, and get the confidence interval from an autocorrelation-aware method or a block bootstrap — naive OLS will understate the interval by a factor of two to three.

## What I'd compute instead

1. **Basin-scale, honestly posed.** Ocean mass trend for the Irminger Basin / subpolar North Atlantic, restricted to mascons whose centers are ≥500 km from the ice margin. That is a resolvable quantity with a defensible uncertainty.
2. **Shelf-scale with explicit leakage correction.** Forward-model the Greenland land signal (from a land-only GRACE inversion or an independent mass-balance product), propagate its gravitational leakage into the ocean mascons, subtract, and carry the correction's own uncertainty. This is the only route to a shelf number worth quoting.
3. **The diagnostic that settles it either way.** Compute the trend as a function of distance from the coast, and with and without CRI. If the value migrates strongly as you step offshore, leakage dominates — and you've converted the caveat into evidence rather than an assertion. I'd run this first regardless of which of the above you choose.

If you want a genuine shelf-scale manometric signal, GRACE is the wrong instrument. Bottom pressure recorders, or tide gauges differenced against altimetry-minus-steric, resolve that scale; GRACE does not.
