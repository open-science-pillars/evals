## Why I'd push back on the question even with the data in hand

This is general domain knowledge, **not** concept-backed, and should be re-checked against the concepts once they're installed:

Southeast Greenland shelf is close to the worst-case region on Earth for this particular product. Three things collide:

- **The region is smaller than the measurement.** JPL mascons are 3° equal-area elements (served on a 0.5° grid, which flatters the true resolution). The SE Greenland shelf is narrower than a single mascon, so "shelf waters" is not a resolvable target — you'd be reading a land–ocean blend. The CRI filter mitigates but does not eliminate this.
- **The largest nearby signal is on land.** Greenland is losing mass at a couple hundred Gt/yr. Residual leakage from that into the adjacent ocean mascons is plausibly larger than the ocean-mass trend you're trying to measure, and it has the same sign as a real ocean mass loss — so the result looks physical and is not.
- **Part of the true signal is also not what it appears.** Greenland's ice loss lowers local sea level via the gravitational fingerprint — reduced self-attraction pulls water away from the coast. So a genuinely negative ocean-mass trend on that shelf is expected and is a *signature of ice loss*, not the shelf gaining or losing water for oceanographic reasons. Leakage and fingerprint are separately negative and hard to disentangle at mascon resolution.

Add GIA uncertainty (large and model-dependent around Greenland, and the correction is already baked into the product) and a 6-year window that is short against interannual variability, and the honest result is a number whose dominant error terms you cannot state.

Your 2019–2024 window does at least sit cleanly **after** the GRACE/GRACE-FO inter-mission gap — `skills/sea-level-budget/SKILL.md` puts that at 2017-07 through 2018-05, so the gap-crossing refusal would not fire.
