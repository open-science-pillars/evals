## What I can tell you, labeled as general method, not concept-sourced

Everything below is my own background knowledge. It is exactly what the bundle exists to supersede, so treat it as a sketch to be replaced, not as a citation:

**The region you picked is close to the worst case for this product.** JPL mascons are roughly 3° equal-area blocks — order 300 km across. The southeast Greenland shelf is narrower than that. The product is *posted* on a 0.5° grid, which fools people constantly: the posting is interpolation, not resolution, so a pretty-looking half-degree map of the shelf carries no independent shelf-scale information. There is essentially no mascon there that is purely ocean.

**The contaminating signal is enormous and has the same sign as the real one.** Greenland has been losing mass at a few hundred Gt/yr. The CRI filter partitions land from ocean inside coastline-straddling mascons, but residual leakage from a source that large is plausibly comparable to or bigger than the ocean-mass trend you're after. Worse: there is a genuine physical signal here — as the ice sheet loses mass its gravitational pull weakens, so relative sea level actually *falls* within roughly a couple thousand km of Greenland. Leakage and real signal both push negative. A result can look physically reasonable and still be mostly leaked ice mass. That is the trap, and it's why "compute it and caveat it" isn't good enough.

**The window is short and badly anchored.** 2019–2024 is about six years of monthly solutions, with gaps in the GRACE-FO record, and it opens on an extreme Greenland melt year. A six-year trend is endpoint-sensitive, and a naive OLS confidence interval on autocorrelated monthly mass data will understate the true uncertainty substantially — this needs an autocorrelation-aware treatment, not a least-squares slope.

**Two corrections I'd need the concept to pin down** rather than recall: which GIA model is baked into the mascons and whether it's applied over ocean cells (GIA is large and model-dependent near formerly glaciated margins like this one), and the dealiasing convention — whether GAD has been restored, which determines whether you're looking at ocean bottom pressure or total ocean mass.

One thing I'd actively warn against: the scale/gain factors shipped with the mascon product are derived from a **land** hydrology model to restore land amplitude. They are not an ocean leakage correction and shouldn't be applied here.
