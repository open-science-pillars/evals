## The substantive problem: this trend isn't measurable with this product

Even with data in hand, "ocean mass trend for the shelf waters around southeast Greenland" is not a quantity JPL mascons can deliver. Three reasons, in order of severity:

1. **The region is smaller than the measurement.** GRACE-FO gravity fields resolve a few hundred km. The JPL mascon product is *distributed* on a 0.5° grid, but that grid is an interpolation of 3° equal-area estimation units (~300 km). A shelf strip off SE Greenland is tens of km wide. There is no independent shelf-ocean estimate inside the product — you'd be reading a smoothed blend of whatever sits within ~300 km.

2. **What sits within 300 km is the fastest-thinning sector of the Greenland Ice Sheet.** Coastal mascons mix land and ocean. JPL applies a Coastline Resolution Improvement filter to split them, but CRI is a partitioning assumption, not a measurement, and it degrades exactly where the land signal is enormous relative to the ocean signal. Here the land term is one of the largest mass trends on Earth and the ocean term is small. A trend computed over those mascons would be mostly ice-sheet leakage wearing an ocean label.

3. **It would look plausible, which is the trap.** Near a rapidly deglaciating ice sheet, the sea-level fingerprint means local ocean mass genuinely *falls* — gravitational attraction toward the ice relaxes as it melts. Leakage from that same ice loss pushes the mascon ocean trend in the *same direction*. You would get a confident negative number that is physically expected and still almost entirely artifact, with no way to apportion it from the product alone. On top of that, the GIA and elastic-uplift corrections are large and model-dependent in this exact region, and 2019–2024 is six years — short enough that interannual variability dominates any regional trend regardless of leakage.

One thing that is *not* a problem: 2019–2024 sits entirely after the GRACE/GRACE-FO inter-mission gap. I read the gap bounds (2017-07 through 2018-05) directly from the sanctioned executor at `skills/sea-level-budget/scripts/sea_level_budget.py:87`, so the bridge refusal wouldn't fire on this window.

There is also no attested path for this: the regional sea-level computation in `sea-level-analysis` binds `region` to a registry of `gulf-of-mexico`, `north-sea`, `us-northeast-coast` and a span of 1992-01 to 2017-12. SE Greenland isn't registered and 2019–2024 is out of span.
