## What the knowledge bundle says

I consulted the installed bundles per `core:consult-knowledge`. The controlling concept is `knowledge/podaac/gotchas/grace-coastal-leakage.md` — **severity high, status stable, human-verified** (PaulMRamirez, 2026-07-04) — and it names this exact case:

> *"Nearshore ocean mass series inherit land signal: apparent coastal sea level or ocean-mass trends near Greenland, Alaska, or major river basins can be dominated by leakage rather than ocean change. Nothing errors; the series just is not what its coordinates claim."*

Two independent failures stack here:

1. **Leakage.** Mascons are 3° blocks; blocks straddling the SE Greenland coast integrate both the ice sheet and the shelf. The CRI filter partitions those blocks into land and ocean parts and "reduces leakage substantially but does not eliminate it." SE Greenland is adjacent to one of the largest mass-loss signals on Earth, so the residual after partitioning is being compared against an ocean signal orders of magnitude smaller.

2. **Resolution.** Per `datasets/grace-fo-mascons.md`, the native information scale is the ~300 km mascon, not the 0.5° grid the data ships on. The SE Greenland shelf is narrower than a single mascon. The dataset concept's own rule: *"a basin whose area approaches or falls below the mascon scale is resolution- and leakage-dominated."*

The gotcha's correct approach — *"keeps a buffer from strong land sources"* — has no valid instantiation here. The strong land source **is** the thing the shelf wraps around.

One genuine point in your favor: 2019–2024 sits entirely within GRACE-FO, so it does not cross the 2017–2018 inter-mission gap (`gotchas/grace-intermission-gap.md`, high). That trap is avoided by your window choice.
