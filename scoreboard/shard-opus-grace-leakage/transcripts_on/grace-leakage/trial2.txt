## I did not produce a trend number, and the reason is the region, not the tooling

`knowledge/podaac/gotchas/grace-coastal-leakage.md` (severity **high**, status **stable**, human-verified 2026-07-04) names your exact case as its wrong-result mode:

> "Nearshore ocean mass series inherit land signal: apparent coastal sea level or ocean-mass trends near Greenland, Alaska, or major river basins can be dominated by leakage rather than ocean change. Nothing errors; the series just is not what its coordinates claim."

The geometry is the problem. Per `knowledge/podaac/datasets/grace-fo-mascons.md` (stable, human-verified 2026-09-13), the JPL solution estimates mass on **4,551 equal-area 3-degree spherical caps** — roughly 110,000 km² each, about 335 km on a side — and is distributed as 0.5° grids that *represent* those mascons: "the native information scale is the mascon, not the grid cell." The southeast Greenland shelf is a strip on the order of 50–150 km wide (general geography, not from a concept). It does not contain a single whole ocean mascon. Every mascon you would touch is a coastline-straddling one whose land half sits under the Helheim–Kangerdlugssuaq sector, among the fastest-losing parts of the ice sheet.

The recipe that owns regional mascon sums, `knowledge/podaac/recipes/grace-mass-to-sea-level.md` (stable), requires the region be "a mask defined at mascon scale (whole mascons, chosen from the placement file), **never at 0.5-degree cell scale**." Your region cannot satisfy that.

**Why this one is especially treacherous.** A naive computation would not return garbage — it would return a strong *negative* ocean-mass trend that looks physically right. Ice-loss leakage pushes the coastal ocean mascon negative, and the real manometric signal near a shrinking ice sheet is also negative (the sea-level fingerprint: reduced gravitational attraction plus solid-Earth uplift depress relative sea level within ~2,000 km of Greenland — standard physics, not from a bundle concept). The artifact and the signal share a sign and a rough magnitude. You would have no way to tell them apart from the number alone, and the leakage term is explicitly *not* in the product's formal errors: the dataset concept states the formal grids "do NOT include the two dominant systematic terms: coastal leakage ... and the GIA model choice ... both exceed the formal errors regionally."
