## The substantive problem with the question

Even fully unblocked, I don't think I could give you a defensible number for *this* quantity, and I'd rather say so than hand you one.

JPL mascons are solved on ~3° equal-area caps — roughly 300 km. They're *distributed* on a 0.5° grid, but that posting is interpolation, not resolution. The shelf strip around southeast Greenland is narrower than a single mascon. There is no "shelf waters" domain to average over; any polygon you draw there is sampling mascons that are substantially land.

That matters more than usual at this specific location, for two independent reasons:

1. **Leakage.** Southeast Greenland sits next to one of the largest and most sharply varying mass-loss signals on Earth. The CRI filter partitions land and ocean within coastal mascons, but residual leakage scales with the adjacent land signal — and here it's enormous. A trend computed over those cells would be dominated by ice loss bleeding into the ocean estimate.
2. **The sea-level fingerprint.** The real manometric signal near a rapidly deglaciating ice sheet is *negative* — reduced gravitational attraction and elastic uplift move water away from the melting mass. Ocean mass near Greenland falls while global ocean mass rises.

So a computed trend there would very likely come out negative, and you'd be unable to separate how much is genuine local ocean mass loss (fingerprint, real and interesting) from how much is Greenland's ice leaking into your ocean cells (artifact). Both push the same direction, which is exactly the situation where a number looks clean and means nothing. Add a 6-year window against strong interannual bottom-pressure variability in the Irminger/Labrador system, and the CI would be wide even before the leakage problem.

## What I'd suggest instead

- **Enlarge the domain**: an Irminger Sea or Labrador Sea basin average, held several mascon-widths off the coast, is a quantity mascons can actually resolve.
- **Keep the fingerprint question but ask it properly**: a fingerprint gradient across the subpolar North Atlantic is measurable; a shelf-strip trend is not.
- **For the shelf specifically**: bottom pressure recorders or a model OBP field are the right instruments. GRACE is the wrong one at that scale.
