## I'm not delivering a shelf ocean-mass trend

Two blockers, one scientific and one environmental.

**The scientific one stands regardless of data access.** `knowledge/podaac/gotchas/grace-coastal-leakage.md` (severity high, stable, human-verified 2026-07-04) names your exact case as its wrong-result mode: *"apparent coastal sea level or ocean-mass trends near Greenland, Alaska, or major river basins can be dominated by leakage rather than ocean change. Nothing errors; the series just is not what its coordinates claim."*

Two things compound there:

1. **The region is below the product's resolution floor.** `knowledge/podaac/datasets/grace-fo-mascons.md` (stable) is explicit that the 0.5° grid *represents* 3° equal-area mascons — 4,551 of them — and "the native information scale is the mascon, not the grid cell." That's ~1.1×10⁵ km² per mascon, ~335 km across (my arithmetic from the concept's figures). The SE Greenland shelf is roughly 900 km alongshore by 50–150 km wide — order 10⁵ km², i.e. **about one mascon or less, for the whole region**. The concept's Uncertainty section: "a basin whose area approaches or falls below the mascon scale is resolution- and leakage-dominated."

2. **Every mascon covering that shelf straddles the coast of the fastest-losing sector of the Greenland Ice Sheet.** The CRI filter partitions straddling mascons "substantially but does not eliminate" the leakage. Order-of-magnitude (my reasoning, not a concept's number): ice loss in those coastal mascons runs tens of cm of equivalent water thickness per year, while a genuine regional ocean-mass trend is sub-cm/yr — roughly two orders of magnitude apart. A few percent leakage residual swamps the ocean signal entirely.

**The environmental one:** this session denied the Bash calls needed to check the Python stack and Earthdata credentials, so I could not have run the computation anyway. That's a separate fact from the above, and it's the smaller of the two.
