## I stopped short of running it, and the reason is the result itself

`knowledge/podaac/gotchas/grace-coastal-leakage.md` (provider tier, **status stable, severity high**, human-verified by PaulMRamirez 2026-07-04) names your exact case as its wrong-result mode:

> "Nearshore ocean mass series inherit land signal: apparent coastal sea level or ocean-mass trends **near Greenland**, Alaska, or major river basins can be dominated by leakage rather than ocean change. Nothing errors; the series just is not what its coordinates claim."

The geometry is the problem, and it isn't fixable by masking harder:

- JPL RL06.3Mv04 solves **4,551 equal-area 3° spherical caps** — roughly 112,000 km² each, about **330 km across**. The 0.5° grid you'd download is a *rendering* of those caps, not independent information (`knowledge/podaac/datasets/grace-fo-mascons.md`, stable, human-verified twice, most recently 2026-09-13).
- The southeast Greenland shelf is roughly **50–150 km wide** over most of that coast, widening toward Kap Farvel. It is comfortably **sub-mascon**. Every mascon with shelf water in it also contains ice sheet.
- So there is no set of *whole ocean mascons* covering the SE Greenland shelf, which is what the recipe requires: "the region is a set of whole mascons taken from the placement file … never at 0.5-degree cell scale" (`knowledge/podaac/recipes/grace-mass-to-sea-level.md`, stable). The gotcha's correct approach — "keeps a buffer from strong land sources or models them explicitly" — has no buffer available here.

## What the number would have looked like, and why I won't hand it to you

You would have gotten a **strongly negative** ocean mass trend, and it would have been read as the shelf losing mass. Order of magnitude, clearly labeled as an argument and not a computed result:

- Greenland's ice loss over 2019–2024 is order **a few hundred Gt/yr**, concentrated within a few hundred km of your box — i.e. inside the same mascons.
- The genuine ocean-mass signal there is the *sea-level fingerprint* of that melt, which is **negative near Greenland** (the ice sheet's gravitational pull on the ocean relaxes as it thins) at order **1–3 mm/yr**, or a few tenths of a cm/yr of equivalent water thickness.

That's a signal-to-leakage ratio of roughly **1:10 to 1:100**. The CRI filter "reduces leakage substantially but does not eliminate it." You'd be measuring the ice sheet through an ocean mask and calling it ocean mass.

Three things specific to your 2019–2024 window that compound it:

1. **2019 was an extreme Greenland melt year** and it sits at the start of the window, so it levers the slope hard — the leakage term is not just large, it's front-loaded.
2. **A July 2025 fix corrected the GAD mass added back to the ocean part of land/ocean mascons**, and only files whose series runs past March 2025 carry it (release note, via the dataset concept). That fix lands on precisely the coastal land/ocean mascons your region is made of.
3. Accelerometer-transplant wide-dead-band months (Jan–Feb 2023, Jul 2023 onward) sit inside your window. The 2017–18 inter-mission gap does *not* — 2019–2024 is entirely GRACE-FO, so that one gotcha is clear.

## What I can do instead — pick one and I'll run it

1. **Bound the leakage explicitly.** Compute the trend over your shelf mascons from *both* the CRI grid (`TELLUS_GRAC-GRFO_MASCON_CRI_GRID_RL06.3_V4`) and the unfiltered grid (`..._MASCON_GRID_RL06.3_V4`). This gives you a defensible statement of the form "the shelf trend is X ± formal, with a leakage term of Y that exceeds it" — which is an honest published-quality result, just not the one you asked for.
2. **Move the box offshore.** Whole ocean mascons in the Irminger Sea / central Denmark Strait, one full mascon clear of the coast, will give a real manometric trend with the fingerprint signal in it. This is the version that survives review.
3. **Reframe to the quantity GRACE actually resolves here** — the SE Greenland *ice sheet* drainage-basin mass trend over 2019–2024, which is a well-posed mascon-scale problem and is probably the signal you're circling anyway.

I'd suggest 1 and 2 together: the offshore trend as the result, the CRI-minus-unfiltered difference as the leakage caveat.
